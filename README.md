# 162-0

A browser baseball challenge: draft sixteen exact historical selections (nine hitters, three starters, a closer, and three named relievers), then play a deterministic 162-game season. SvelteKit 3, Svelte 5, Tailwind 4, and Cloudflare Workers Static Assets. Season simulation and share-image capture run in the browser; the Worker only checks and stores replays and published images. No user account or server database is required; sharing uses the configured R2 object store.

Planned shared simulation and Draft Mode work is organized in the [design index](design/README.md), with authoritative system rules and proposed implementation phases.

Draft progress, fielding/DH assignments, and lineup order are saved on the device. Exact season selections are permanent; qualifying position players can move into an empty slot or swap reciprocally before simulation. Moves preserve a pending roll and must leave the roster completable. Replay schema 5 with model `contact-v1` and rules `classic-v2` is the only supported gameplay contract. Sharing stores versioned inputs, including the chosen home stadium and its geometry version, plus the authoritative roll/pick/reassignment action history under an opaque replay ID; the recipient validates and recomputes that history, while the Worker checks only its shape, versions, size, and current `dataVersion` (from the small `/data/current.json` pointer) so each request stays within the Workers Free CPU limit. The `REPLAYS` R2 bucket stores replay inputs and immutable publication assets, not accounts or request metadata. Incompatible saves and links remain detectable, explain the beta cutover, and require a new draft instead of being silently reinterpreted.

A new draft starts by choosing a home stadium from a deck of the 30 current MLB parks, compiled from `scripts/data/stadiums.json`. Each stadium is a card with the park's photo and line and center distances on the front; turning it over shows the field outline, wall heights, roof, elevation, and photo credit, with estimated measurements labelled. The draft's 81 home games are played there; road games use each 2025 opponent's home park. A stadium has no rating or bonus and affects play only through ball flight. The deck sorts by park or club name, and starred favorites are pinned first; both are remembered per browser.

Draft cards select a candidate without saving a pick. Choose a highlighted field destination, review the exact season preview, then confirm with Draft. Turning a candidate card raises its reverse into a centered reading surface. Field-card review opens centered on the front; the turn symbol beneath it shows the back. Turning back, Escape, or clicking outside returns the card to its original slot without moving the field. Move first selects a hitter for movement, then opens review on a second click. Review first opens review immediately and offers Move card on the back. Phones and portrait tablets use a field sheet with Field/Card back tabs and pinned confirmation. Text version belongs to the reverse face and presents the same statistics and source credits in readable text, without supplemental estimates or model explanations. Shared rules and model assumptions live on Rules & model. Clicking outside cards, field slots, and action controls discards only the preview; qualifying roster moves and swaps apply immediately.

Candidate ordering follows each displayed exact season: batting or pitching WAR/162 by default, or OPS and ERA. Changing a season reorders the cards without committing a pick. Desktop candidate fronts lead with artwork and one season/placement/turn control row, without repeated identity headings. Text version is available on the turned candidate. Centered pagination contains the page numbers and total player count, with explicit disabled boundaries; changing pages brings the new cards into view. Ranking controls use short labels, with method details available through Rules & model. Season controls and position buttons stay within each card, and complete names refit when card widths or fonts change.

## Interface and results

Home uses real canonical profiles for its drifting card wall and eight-era showcase, plus all thirty franchise marks. Desktop era cards use four fitted columns and two chronological rows, up to 128px by default, in a panel to the right of the copy from 68rem; phones keep the same two rows of four at up to 80px. The showcase has no scrolling rail. Roster, staff, and lineup cards use the canonical compact treatment: the same eight decade fronts receive era-specific plates and natural name wrapping. The draft field is a card printed from the home stadium's measured outline and scaled to its container: 56-unit artwork with full-name captions on a 424-unit card, so all nine hitters and the five-person staff fit the desktop panel and the phone sheet without scrolling sideways. Other compact collections retain a 72px artwork minimum.

Lineup rows show each player's order number, compact card, and historical stats used to set the order (OBP/SLG/HR for hitters, ERA/SO/IP for starters, ERA/SO/SV for relievers). The order number is the mouse/touch drag handle within batting order or starting rotation. The dragged row follows the pointer and the other rows slide aside to preview the drop; only a completed drop saves the order. Escape or dropping outside the list cancels. Labelled up/down buttons remain available for keyboard ordering, and the closer and RP1–RP3 stay fixed. Tapping a compact card raises it from its row for review, as on the draft field. Hide card restores the activating control without changing selections. Fielding/DH changes apply as soon as a legal slot is chosen, retaining qualification-aware moves and reciprocal swaps.

Draft loading uses eight era-styled TEAM placeholders, with one card plucked upward out of the board. These are not eligible-player cards or fabricated selections. The animation pauses offscreen or while the page is hidden and remains static when Motion is off or reduced motion is requested.

Results preserve the complete batting and pitching totals and all 162 game boxes, reached through a pocket-schedule calendar or a by-opponent season-series view. The awards spread considers every participating individual and features MVP, batting title, fewest runs allowed (RA9), strikeout leader, and LVP; the pooled BP remains in team accounting but is not an individual award candidate. Exact unrounded ties receive chips, one stable winner is featured per category, and multiple awards merge onto one card. Inspection switches between the simulated 162-0 season and the recorded actual season, with hitter and pitcher cohorts ranked separately; missing or invalid source values are unavailable, not zero.

Device saves live in a SQLite database (`@sqlite.org/sqlite-wasm`) on the origin private file system, opened by a dedicated worker through the `opfs-sahpool` VFS, which needs no cross-origin isolation. `src/lib/storage/migrations.ts` brings it to the current schema on open; each step runs in one transaction with its `user_version` bump, so a failed step leaves the previous version intact, and a database from a newer release is refused. The first step imports the earlier localStorage saves (`162-zero:v1`, `162-zero:library:v2`, `162-zero:series:v1`), skipping malformed entries, and removes those keys once the import is on disk. The pool admits one tab at a time: a second tab, or a browser without OPFS, plays with a memory database and says its saves will not persist. Settings, the stadium deck order, and an accepted challenge stay in localStorage. A Draft Mode run is saved as its setup (seed, franchise and the rules, salary, data and run-format versions) plus the player's action log, and resumes by replaying that log; a run saved under any other version is refused with the reason rather than reinterpreted.

`/collection` is the binder: every player-season the player has put on a finished team, kept even after its season is deleted from the library, with how often it was drafted, its best team record and that season's line. It can be grouped by team or era and shows completion against every individual player-season in the game data; team bullpens are collected but do not count toward completion. Cards earn stickers (`src/lib/collection/stickers.ts`, `stickers-v1`) from their saved season lines and team records: 40 and 60 HR, 200 hits, 130 RBI, 30 steals, a .400 OBP over 502 PA, 400 and 600 strikeouts, a starter under 2.50 RA9 over 162 IP, a closer under 2.00 RA9 over 30 IP, team MVP, 100-win, 120-win and 162–0 teams, and being drafted five and ten times. Thresholds sit near the top decile and top two percent of simulated seasons. Each sticker is earned once per player-season, lands at a default spot, and can be moved or hidden on its own card. Results lists the stickers a season earned for the first time. Stickers are cosmetic: they never enter a draft, a replay or the simulation.

A complete season earns a grade from fixed win bands (`src/lib/game/grade.ts`): Perfect at 162 wins, S+ 150–161, S 144–149, AA 136–143, A 125–135, B 110–124, C 90–109, D 70–89, F 69 or fewer. S is reachable by skilled drafting alone; S+ also needs a lucky season. Results, the share image, library clubs and each collected card's best team show it.

Every completed season of the player's own draft is saved automatically, separate from the draft in progress. Each entry keeps the replay, record, runs, roster, MVP, and every drafted player's simulated season line. `/seasons` shows them as club tiles with all-time leaderboards, per-club cards and stats, and a two-club tale of the tape. Every season is kept until deleted. Entries can be renamed or deleted. Full season results opens the entry's results page from its inline `#replay=` link. Copy challenge link publishes through `POST /api/replays` and copies the short `/r/{id}` link, falling back to the inline `#replay=` link. Entries from an older game version show as retired: roster, record and stats stay visible, but they cannot play head-to-head. Every finished series is recorded (newest first, a replayed series kept once and moved to the top) with both clubs' library keys, so each club shows its head-to-head tally and past meetings. Home links to My seasons and Head-to-head; Results offers Play head-to-head.

`/h2h` takes two same-site replay links (`/r/{id}` or `/#replay=`), or two entries from the library, and plays a best-of-five in the browser. Both regular seasons are recomputed to seed the series: more wins is seed 1; equal records give seed 1 to the replay whose canonical key sorts first, and identical replays give it to Team A. The 2-2-1 format has seed 1 hosting games 1, 2, and 5 on calendar days 1, 2, 4, 5, and 7, each at the host's chosen home stadium. Rotations cycle the three starters and closer workload carries across days. The series seed derives from the unordered pair of replay keys, so the same two links always give the same games (rules `h2h-bo5-v1`, seed policy `replay-pair-v1`). Game and series MVP (`realized-runs-v1`) is the highest unrounded batting + baserunning + defense + pitching runs above neutral on either team, excluding the pooled support bullpen; ties use the canonical key, then season ID. Series superlatives (`realized-runs-awards-v1`) rank one component each with the MVP tie rule: Top bat (batting runs, among batters), Ace (pitching runs, among pitchers other than the support bullpen), Glove (defensive runs, among fielders), Wheels (baserunning runs, among batters), and LVP (lowest total). Glove and Wheels are omitted when the leader is under 0.05 runs. Highlights and lowlights are the largest win-expectancy swings from Team A's side. The series link `/h2h#a=<link>&an=<name>&b=<link>&bn=<name>` recomputes on open; the server stores nothing about a series; the device keeps its head-to-head history.

Season in review and game boxes share a dense scorebook treatment: compact name/year identities, tabular numeric alignment, alternating row grounds, grouped statistics, and a sticky identity column inside labelled horizontal scroll regions. Every statistic remains available on phones. The game log uses two columns on wide screens; expanded boxes span both columns.

Exported season artwork uses static SVG field geometry, flat card compositing, and a font/image/text-fit readiness gate before capture. Repeated captures of the same publication must produce identical PNG bytes.

Settings contains Motion, Card review animation, Card speed, and Roster first click. All four preferences persist locally in `162-zero:settings:v1`; denied storage leaves the controls usable for the session. Motion governs movement and transitions throughout the game. Card review animation controls card travel and rotation independently; Card speed ranges from 0.5× to 2×, with a default 1× duration of 780ms. System reduced motion overrides both animation switches without overwriting saved preferences. Ambient motion uses a fixed amount of 2.5× and speed of 1×. One visibility-aware scheduler drives wall rows, card drift/finish lighting, spotlights, and marquees only while subscribers are visible.

Card text fitting batches font writes and layout reads across unique labels, including SVG arcs. Resize and text changes use geometry keys; font loading and option changes force fresh measurement.

## Run

Use Node >=24.12 and npm. Install with `npm ci`, then `npx playwright install chromium` for headless browser tests. The simulation engine is Rust: install [rustup](https://rustup.rs) (it reads the pinned toolchain and wasm target from `rust-toolchain.toml`) and `cargo install wasm-pack`. Every hook that needs the engine runs `just engine`, which rebuilds the wasm package into the ignored `src/lib/engine/pkg/` only when the Rust sources change. Browser tests run the built app on local workerd with file watching disabled; `PLAYWRIGHT_PORT` selects an isolated port.

```sh
just dev --host 127.0.0.1 --port 5173
just engine
just test
just smoke --seed 162 --policy best
just calibrate --seed 162 --games 10000
just bench --runs 5
just draft-feasibility --seeds 64
just hill-climb --budget-minutes 60
just hill-climb-replay --seed 162
```

`just bench` prints median timings for policy drafts and a full wasm season, then the native engine's preparation, matchup fitting, and full and fast season timings on the same roster, plus a fingerprint of the seed-162 season result. An optimization that should not change simulated values must leave the fingerprint unchanged. `just test` also runs `cargo test --workspace`, whose search test reads the prepared dataset.

`just hill-climb` searches natively for the Classic roster with the most expected wins, ignoring the draft rolls: any sixteen distinct franchises and players, each eligible at its slot, plus a home stadium and both orders. It starts from the best draft-pricing assignment of slots to franchises in the stadium that screens best, then runs an iterated local search (replacements from each slot's top candidates, position, stadium, batting-order and rotation swaps). Proposals are screened on part of a fixed 64-season seed panel and accepted only when the paired mean-win gain's 95% lower bound over the whole panel is positive, with perturbation restarts after a stall. The best roster is then scored on fresh holdout seeds: mean wins with standard error, the win histogram, the best season and its seed, the count of 162-0 seasons, and an estimated P(162-0) from per-game win rates by opponent, venue and rotation spot (assuming independent games). The checkpoint (`--resume`) and `report.json` go to `.cache/hill-climb/`; `just hill-climb-replay --seed N` replays one full season of the reported roster and lists its losses.

`just draft-feasibility` is the headless Draft Mode D01 proof. It needs no browser or profile chunks. For each seed and pick policy, it deals each of eight seats two eight-card packs of real player-seasons (one gold-or-gem slot, two silver-or-better slots and five common-or-better slots per pack, upgraded at the pool's natural finish rates). Each seat cracks its packs into one 16-card pile; seats take one card and pass the pile left until all 128 cards are drafted, so every pile visits every seat twice. Rosters have 18 active roles (Classic's 16 plus RP4 and RP5; every named relief slot takes any card that could close) and two reserves. It drafts with the Draft Mode AI general manager (`ai`) or stand-in stress policies, then runs opening recovery: cuts, then cleanup from each seat's own unlimited per-role stacks of commons, of which only the top card is visible. It checks every submission against the cap and roster rules. Each seeded table seats eight franchises as a player run would, so AI clubs score with their home parks. AI clubs play at `--difficulty rookie|pro|ace` (default `pro`), and the `ai` policy seats one perfect drafter (true values, no bias) at seat 0 and reports where its submitted roster ranks. It also reports how often AI clubs take the highest finish in a pile and how many picks park fit decided, runs named negative fixtures and replays every run. A one-screen summary goes to stdout, the full JSON report goes to `.cache/draft-feasibility/report.json`, and the exit code is 1 when any gate fails. `--policy ai|value|needs|lopsided|random|mixed|all`, `--difficulty`, `--seed-start` and `--report` select the sweep.

Browser tests start their own local Worker/R2 surface on port 4162. If another process owns it, use `PLAYWRIGHT_PORT=4172 just test` (or another free port); the suite never reuses an existing server. Before starting it, `scripts/test-build.ts` runs the prepare steps and rebuilds only when a content hash of the build inputs (application source and build scripts, excluding tests; hand-authored static files; generated-asset pointers; root configuration) differs from the last suite build. `FORCE_BUILD=1` forces a rebuild.

Normal automated tests use the local Worker, local R2 surface, and local browser fixtures.

Development and builds prepare the statistical, ranking, and image assets automatically. The first preparation needs network access and ImageMagick's `magick` executable for image conversion. Raw statistics and ranking exports are checksum-verified and cached under ignored `.cache/lahman/` and `.cache/rankings/`; source metadata and thumbnails are cached under ignored `.cache/media/`. Generated data and imagery belong under ignored `static/data/`, `static/rankings/`, and `static/media/`, never in source control. Synthetic test identities contain no public athlete names.

The start page loads gameplay and phase-specific components on demand. Tailwind supplies theme and reset layers without scanning generated assets for utilities; Vite excludes generated statistical, ranking, and media directories from file watching.

```sh
npm run data:prepare
npm run data:prepare -- --offline
npm run rankings:prepare
npm run rankings:prepare -- --offline
npm run media:prepare
npm run media:prepare -- --offline
npm run build
npx wrangler deploy --dry-run
just deploy
```

Production runs at https://162-0.dev on the `162-zero` Worker. `just deploy` publishes the application and its custom-domain binding; Cloudflare manages DNS and TLS. The existing workers.dev endpoint remains available.

Share publication requires the existing `REPLAYS` R2 binding and `PUBLIC_ORIGIN` set to the canonical deployment origin. `wrangler.jsonc` uses compatibility date `2026-10-05`. No Browser Rendering binding or Worker secret is needed, and every replay and share request fits the Workers Free plan's 10 ms CPU limit.

The rankings index pins the same source revision and SHA-256 as the statistical compiler. A season whose source rows cover fewer scheduled games than the shortest completed season in the covered window is published as unavailable rather than ranked from a fraction of a season.

The compiler also writes a versioned `draft-cards.json` (`salary-runs-v1`, provisional) for Draft Mode. It prices each candidate at the modeled season runs above its role's replacement level: shared-engine linear weights against a league-average opponent in the neutral park, plus fielding runs at the position, over the role's workload. The replacement level is the 20th percentile of eligible cards. Baserunning, park fit and platoon effects are not priced. Cosmetic finishes come from WAR and never enter the price. Classic does not load this file. Beside it, `draft-park-{franchise}.json` holds each card's park fit (`park-fit-v1`) for that franchise's 2025 home park: the card's contact fit is played out in the park and in the neutral park, the same change for a league-average player in the role is subtracted, and the difference is scaled to the card's priced workload over the home half of the season, in tenths of a run. Positive means the card plays up there. Draft Mode AI clubs value cards with their own park's fit; it is not part of the salary.

The data compiler pins an immutable third-party transport commit and SHA-256 for every source CSV. Changed or missing bytes fail rather than selecting another release. Output URLs use a SHA-256 version of canonical transformed payloads. The versioned `diagnostics.json` records exclusions and estimated-field counts without display names.

## Images

Team marks identify real franchises in the interface and exported season share images. Reviewed Wikimedia Commons sources retain their documented copyright status. Explicitly reviewed non-free marks retain their source and copyright disclosure and rely on an asserted fair-use basis, not a reuse licence or established permission. This policy applies to every club mark; attribution and non-affiliation notices are not permission. Portraits and atmosphere photographs still require documented reusable sources. The game provides no standalone logo download or card-printing feature.

All 30 current franchise identities have reviewed marks. Historical marks apply only within their verified year ranges; other marks are labelled as current franchise identity. The Toronto current mark is the bird, not a wordmark; the Marlins franchise key `FLA` identifies the current Miami mark. Unavailable marks retain an explicit abbreviation fallback. Reviewed round source marks retain circular frames; square/freeform marks use square frames without cropping the artwork.

Normal media compilation consumes reviewed sources. Broad discovery runs explicitly with `just media-discover`; `--providers=cached,commons,openverse,loc`, `--player=ID`, `--missing-only`, `--limit=N`, `--requests=N`, `--refresh`, and `--offline` control a resumable run. Missing players are ordered by selectable cards restored, followed by players lacking same-decade MLB photographs. Cached Wikidata identities (`P1825`), portraits (`P18`), player categories (`P373`), name aliases, Commons structured subjects, and `--collection="Commons category"` supply leads. Openverse and Library of Congress searches preserve provider pagination and source links. A blocked or interrupted search never counts as an exhausted search. TCDB is a manual research reference, not an automated image provider.

`just media-review --limit=100 --per-player=2 --batch=review-name` prepares downloaded contact sheets, source revisions, byte checksums, and a queue under ignored `.cache/media/review/`. Sheets explicitly label every image as an unreviewed search candidate; the command never grants approval. Baseball-context and player-specific leads precede broad name-only matches; those weak leads remain available for review. `--players=ID,ID` selects specific players; `--all` includes covered players; `--legacy-only` audits the currently published unclassified sources; `--evidence-only` preserves an existing queue while obtaining its source revisions. Each new entry in `scripts/media/photo-reviews.json` records identity, uniform, playing/later context, underlying rights, visual review, source metadata, source checksum, and any normalized crop. A stable provider-qualified source ID also supports direct institutional/archive entries outside the discovery providers. Only documented public domain, CC0, CC BY, and CC BY-SA qualify. Photographic card extracts and identified individual crops qualify when the underlying work's rights are documented; a license on a scan alone does not suffice. Rejections retain their evidence and reason. A name match or collection membership never verifies identity.

Query pages and downloads are checksum-verified and cached. Acquisition respects provider pacing and retries interrupted transfers; authentication failures and service-wide denials stop further requests to that provider. Openverse leads require rights verification at the original source ([Openverse guidance](https://docs.openverse.org/api/reference/made_with_ov.html)). LOC discovery uses its [public metadata API](https://www.loc.gov/apis/json-and-yaml/). Offline preparation rebuilds approved sources from local caches. Source snapshots, approvals and crop changes invalidate prepared outputs; prepared builds verify every published image's content hash. The development watcher ignores acquisition caches and generated data, ranking, and media trees while retaining source updates.

Schema-3 portraits prefer playing-era MLB uniforms from the card's decade, other MLB playing photos, minor-league playing uniforms, other playing uniforms, then later coaching or old-timers uniforms. Within a tier, the nearest evidenced date wins, then the drafted franchise, image quality, and stable source ID. Same-year alternatives remain available. Capture dates may be exact, approximate, a range, or unknown; unknown dates rank last within their tier. Publication/upload dates never become capture dates, and MLB debut/retirement dates do not exclude a reviewed minor-league or later photograph. Cards, portrait components and reporting use the same ranking. Composite bullpen units never receive a player's portrait.

Schema-2 manifests remain readable. Previously published inventory is retained as explicitly unclassified legacy coverage until reviewed; legacy approval does not establish uniform context. `static/media/previous.json` and prior version directories preserve local rollback. Generated data, rankings and media bypass Vite: after `vite build`, `scripts/package-assets.ts` clones them into `.svelte-kit/cloudflare` (copy-on-write where the filesystem supports it), keeping only the current and previous media versions and their referenced dependencies. Older versions stay in `static/media` as local rollback archives. All image URLs are local and content-hashed. Portraits/marks are bounded to 384px and atmosphere to 1280px without upscaling; small recognizable images are retained. Card text versions retain source, creator, and licence credits; `/about#image-credits` also exposes date/context labels and review evidence. Missing, loading and failed images never block gameplay. Atmosphere is context, not a simulated historical venue. Stadium cards use the franchise's atmosphere photo from `scripts/media/atmosphere-sources.json` (the lowest id when a franchise has several); a park without one shows a placeholder.

`just media-audit` writes coverage by player, selectable card, decade, franchise and preference tier to ignored `.cache/media/audit/`. Its preserved baseline supports before/after comparisons and explicit coverage-loss explanations. Per-player reports list discovered candidates, review blockers and completed, blocked or pending provider searches, including the remaining targeted archive/web review. An unfinished review or search remains pending; it is not proof that a reusable image does not exist.

Record targeted archive/web searches with `node scripts/research-media.ts record --record=FILE.json`. The JSON array contains `{playerId, search}` entries; each search supplies `status` (`complete`, `pending`, or `blocked`), nonempty `queries` and `evidenceUrls`, `attempted: true`, an ISO `updatedAt`, and a `reason` for any blocker. These records document research, never image approval.

No Baseball-Reference subscription or API account is used. [Sports Reference does not provide a data API](https://www.sports-reference.com/bot-traffic.html); its site content and paid tools are not an image republication licence.

This is a non-commercial game intended for public sharing. Free documented-reuse assets are the default; no paid assets are purchased. [MLBAM's notice](https://gdx.mlb.com/components/copyright.txt) permits individual, non-commercial, non-bulk use without defining those terms. [MLB's general terms](https://www.mlb.com/official-information/terms-of-use#section-1) separately limit the download exception to personal, non-commercial home use. Public in-game headshot permission has not been established; individual fetching does not settle display rights. [Hall of Fame game use](https://baseballhall.org/discover-more/photo-archives/image-rights-reproduction) requires a quote, and [Alamy's game-inclusive licence](https://www.alamy.com/image-license-details/) starts at £300 per asset, outside the $25 total research ceiling. Personal-use stock prices are not public-game permission.

Draft ordering and cosmetic card finishes use the JEFFBAGWELL historical WAR file published at [Neil-Paine-1/MLB-WAR-data-historical](https://github.com/Neil-Paine-1/MLB-WAR-data-historical), an MIT-licensed, pinned, checksum-verified download that averages Baseball-Reference and FanGraphs WAR. Its batting/pitching WAR/162 scalar is comparison context only and never strengthens a simulated matchup. The same pinned source also supplies a separate `fld162` fielding-runs column used by `defense-v2`; `pos162`, `def162`, and the composite WAR scalar are not defensive gameplay inputs. Missing values remain unavailable rather than becoming zero.

## Model and data

One Rust engine (`engine/`) plays every game. It is compiled to wasm for the browser's season and series workers, Node scripts, and data preparation (`engine-wasm/`, loaded through `src/lib/engine/`), and natively for benches and search (`engine-cli/`). Transcendental functions come from `libm` rather than the platform, so wasm and native builds produce identical results. Inputs are validated against the TypeScript data contract in `src/lib/sim/` before they cross into the engine as JSON.

AL/NL franchise history from 1950–2025 is translated into a common combined 2025 batting-event environment. Three-year batting and pitching park factors remove the source park from historical rates; simulated venues act only through ball flight. Historical source league priors, coarse park factors, generic platoon effects, inferred pitcher allowed extra-base hits, and explicit runner-advancement probabilities are approximations, not measured historical splits or a professional simulator. Missing sacrifice flies retain available records and yield conservative plate-appearance components; labelled OBP/OPS denominators are not reconstructed historical totals. Position-specific defense combines Lahman fielding evidence with the pinned source's `fld162`: exact-position errors, double plays, assists, catcher SB/CS and fielding outs are smoothed against year/league/position cohorts. Generic outfield evidence is a half-strength fallback only when split LF/CF/RF evidence is absent. DP and assist rates are context-affected proxies, not true opportunity rates, range, throwing velocity, framing, blocking, or proprietary scouting grades.

Each of three selected starters receives 54 starts. With a lead of 0–3 runs, the closer takes the ninth or later (even over a starter still going), RP1 the eighth and RP2 the seventh once the starter is done; RP3 takes the first other relief inning, including the rest of an inning a starter leaves at the out budget. Each named pitcher works at most one inning a game, rests after two straight days, has a season cap of the source relief outs scaled to 162 games, and is skipped when unavailable. Every other relief inning goes to the remainder arm (BP): the pooled, BFP-weighted 2025 league relief, the same for every club, with unlimited workload. Each 2025 opponent uses its saves leader as closer, its next three relievers by outs as named relievers, and the rest of its relief as its remainder. Neutral handedness and unlimited remainder workload are arcade abstractions, not reconstructed relief-only innings. Team-season bullpen cards from earlier versions stay in saved seasons and the collection but are not drafted. The schedule draws actual 2025 opposition but is not an official club schedule. All 162 games run, including after a loss.

The schedule plays five games against every opponent plus a sixth against twelve of them, as 60 two-, three-, and four-game series: every opponent hosts one series and visits for another, the venues balance at 81 home and 81 away, and no opponent plays two series in a row. The results calendar dates the season from a Thursday opening day with 24 off days at travel breaks and three seed-drawn makeup doubleheaders; dates are presentation only and do not affect play. Opponent starters cycle through their five-player rotations. Closers may enter one half inning from the ninth onward when tied or leading by one to three, require three remaining seasonal outs, and rest after appearing in both immediately preceding challenge games. Other relief identifies the drafted team remainder in game boxes and season totals. Only schema 5 / `contact-v1` / `classic-v2` is supported; older replay schemas and model/data combinations are rejected rather than migrated or run through a legacy engine.

Each plate appearance samples BB, HBP, SO, or contact from the platoon-adjusted matchup. Contact draws exit velocity, launch angle, and spray from the 2025 Statcast distribution as continuous values. The compiler fits each matchup's contact mix and power shift so that a neutral reference park (330/380/400/380/330 ft, 10 ft walls, 500 ft elevation, `neutral-reference-v2`) reproduces its park-adjusted singles, doubles plus triples, home runs, and outs; triples are best-effort and fits outside tolerance are counted in diagnostics. Flight uses drag and lift with air density from venue elevation and a per-ball carry factor, in fixed calm conditions with retractable roofs open. A home run must clear the actual wall height at its fence bearing. Hits and outs come from a fielder-reach model: fixed starting positions, reaction, speed, catch height, and throws, fitted to 2025 Statcast outcomes. There is no park-factor multiplier at the venue. `src/lib/sim/park.ts`, `flight.ts`, `fielding.ts`, and `contact-profile.ts` hold the model, and the compiler's `APPROXIMATIONS` list states its limits.

Plate appearances account for inherited runners, forced advancement, errors, steals, double plays, and sacrifice flies. Extra innings begin with empty bases. Non-home-run walkoffs stop on the winning run and truncate hit credit to the winning runner's advance; home runs count every runner. A 1,000-PA half-inning or 100-inning bound raises an error instead of inventing a winner. Results retain all inning lines and batting/pitching boxes; worker progress never controls simulation randomness.

On contact, the physical model decides which fielder plays the ball. A bounded, residualized hit-prevention skill changes that fielder's speed and reaction time; outfield throwing changes throw speed. Error avoidance, infield double-play participation, outfield throwing, and catcher throwing then use their applicable evidence independently. Missing evidence is explicitly neutral; it is not an invented league-history talent rating. `DEF est.` on a hitter front is the pre-season, assigned-position estimate of runs saved per 1,458 reference innings. An unavailable aggregate estimate shows — even when independently evidenced component skills still operate. DH fronts retain HR, while pitchers and BP retain WAR/162 · ERA · SO.

Results separately report realized run value. `sim-war-v2` gives hitters batting value + running value + defensive value + 20 replacement runs per 600 PA, and individual pitchers pitching value + 20 replacement runs per 600 outs, then divides by ten runs per estimated win. Values are not clamped at zero. Participation includes batting, pitching, fielding, and running work; the pooled BP remainder arm contributes to conservation/team accounting but has no individual estimated WAR. This context-dependent app estimate is not historical JEFFBAGWELL, fWAR, bWAR, proprietary-equivalent WAR, or a forecast of team wins.
Season highlights and lowlights are the actual steal, caught-stealing, or plate-appearance events with the largest positive and negative change in the challenge team's neutral win probability. The win model uses the combined league event rates, actual inning/half, outs, occupied bases, and score, but intentionally removes player, pitcher, team, and park strength. Its absorbing half-inning Markov model uses the physical resolver's neutral league transitions in the neutral reference park, including errors, double plays, tag-ups, and runner advancement. Future steals are omitted from the projection even though actual simulated steals are measured as moments. Remaining half-innings and innings are convolved numerically; propagation stops only below 1e-13 remaining mass and rejects failed convergence or invalid mass. An equal-strength tie after regulation or the current extra inning is worth 0.5, with no ghost runner. A completed or skipped half produces an exact 1 or 0 rather than an estimated final-score story.

The game publishes runs allowed per nine innings (RA9) and outs-based baseball innings, not simulated ERA, wins, or saves whose official scoring is not reconstructed. Share actions create a replay ID, then the browser renders the canonical Svelte share artwork off screen in a 1080px query container, rasterizes two 1080×1920 PNGs (the field layout and the cards layout) with `modern-screenshot`, and uploads them with the season record. The Worker checks each PNG's signature and dimensions, the 8 MiB cap, and that the record adds up to 162 games; it does not recompute the season. The replay link is released only after both content-addressed images are stored and the immutable publication manifest is committed (the first publication for a replay wins); subsequent actions reuse those exact bytes, and crawler metadata uses the field image. Challenge a friend shares `/c/{id}`, a challenge card with the record to beat, the published image, Draft your team, and a See their season link to `/r/{id}`; Copy link shares `/r/{id}`, which replays the season itself. Accepting a challenge stores it under `162-zero:challenge:v1` and binds it to a draft's seed: a new draft by default, or, when the device has an unfinished draft, the player's choice of Answer with my current draft (which resumes it via `/?resume=1`) or Start a fresh draft. When that draft's season finishes, Results leads with the comparison and Play the best-of-five, which opens `/h2h` with the new club as Team A and the challenger as Team B. Before publication Results shows a labelled local preview. Capture failure preserves the replay and offers retry; clipboard denial offers a selectable link or PNG download rather than false success. This is friendly deterministic sharing, not a tamper-proof leaderboard.
