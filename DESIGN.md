# Design

## Direction

Broadcast across the complete game: start, draft, lineup, simulation, results, and rules. A baseball fan uses a phone in a dim room during an evening broadcast, so the surface stays dark but uses cool charcoal and slate, not warm brown. Crisp cool-white type and recognizable team identity provide contrast.

This is a product interface, not a marketing dashboard. The next legal choice, the exact historical season, and the collected roster take priority over decoration. Drafting is dense; start and results have room for atmosphere. Preserve the complete game across screen sizes rather than maintaining separate mobile and desktop products.

## References

ESPN broadcast scorebugs for condensed score hierarchy and clear game states; MLB The Show roster presentation for player identity, portraits, and compact comparison. Borrow composition principles, not proprietary layouts or artwork.

The supplied draft references establish the compact desktop composition and cards-only narrow browsing. Treat their layout as reference, not their demo data or simplified eligibility logic. Production cards, engine legality, full identities, source disclosures, and keyboard behavior remain authoritative.

## Color Strategy

Restrained global palette with purposeful franchise-colour identity panels. The shared interface tokens live in `src/routes/layout.css`; card-specific colour roles live in `src/lib/cards/tokens.ts`. Reuse these roles instead of adding local approximations.

Team colours identify the current roll and opponent, not every control. Resolve team-ground foregrounds through the card colour-role helper; do not assume white contrasts with every franchise. Verify rendered text and control contrast; unavailable status also needs words, not opacity alone.

Buttons use the cards' condensed display face through `--font-control`, `--control-size` and `--control-tracking`. Components style controls through these tokens.

## Typography and Composition

Home leads directly with the challenge and draft instructions. Keep the shared wordmark unaccompanied by a tagline; omit decorative eyebrow slogans and motivational captions. Section headings stand alone: no eyebrow above them and no explanatory sub-line beside them; a note stays only when it defines a term the section uses. Small labels identify actual controls, eras, statistics, or game state.

System sans for interface labels, body, controls, and scoreboard-style results. Exact-season card artwork follows the supplied eight decade-specific fronts and backs at 5:7, using self-hosted Barlow Condensed and Roboto Serif. The selected season determines the clamped decade, never the photograph year. Card dimensions and text fitting scale with width; names may wrap but never truncate, ellipsize, or stretch horizontally. Team marks remain labelled historical or current-franchise identity. Hitter fronts show historical WAR/162, OPS, and position-specific `DEF est.`; assigned DH keeps HR. Pitcher and bullpen fronts retain WAR/162 · ERA · SO, with BP WAR unavailable. Inspection backs show historical statistics, ranks, source credits, and licence. Estimated or incomplete counts use the unavailable marker rather than impersonating measurements.

The shared type scale is 12, 14, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48, and 64px (`--text-xs` through `--text-score`). Body copy uses 16px with 1.5 line height and a 68ch maximum paragraph width. Labels and dense table data use 12–14px. Tabular numerals align seasons, scores, and statistical columns. Interface controls remain system sans; Barlow Condensed italic 900 is reserved for compact broadcast headings and team identity. Roboto Serif and the card type treatments belong to the decade templates.

Card stock, kraft, masks, and printed trim retain the supplied era compositions. Team grounds use the published franchise colour; stock is the secondary colour because the runtime manifest publishes no second team colour. Normal text roles reach 4.5:1 and the 1970s headline reaches 3:1. When neither paper nor ink reaches 4.5:1 on a team ground, adjust the stronger foreground toward white or black without changing that ground. Raised photo placeholders retain their striped ground and use an ink that reaches 4.5:1 against both the ground and its light stripe.

Base, Foil, Foil + emboss, and Gem are fixed cosmetic finishes. Hitter/starter historical WAR/162 cut points are 2, 4, and 6; closer cut points are 0.5, 1.5, and 2.5. An individual two-way season uses the higher finite historical WAR across both roles independently of the front's represented role. Bullpen units and wholly unavailable WAR use Base. Historical WAR/162 controls list ordering and finish only; it never alters matchup strength. Separately sourced position-specific defensive evidence is a gameplay input and is disclosed independently. Verified-media selection and labelled failures remain authoritative; no mockup assets substitute for runtime media.

### Spacing and elevation

The spacing scale is 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, and 64px (`--space-1` through `--space-16`). Controls use a 4px radius; ordinary panels use 8px. Use one-pixel boundaries and tonal grounds rather than a shadow on every container. Keep actual baseball cards separate from application panels.

The native field sheet uses an ambient shadow at `oklch(8% .01 255 / .52)` with a 64px blur and upward 24px offset. Its dark backdrop separates modal content from the inert page. Card review fronts stay inline; turned cards use the native top layer without adding a backdrop, scroll lock, or page inertness. Their source slot remains reserved. Card stock, foil, emboss, gem, tilt, and flip depth remain part of the card-rendering system rather than generic interface elevation.

### Control vocabulary

- Primary actions use the accent ground and background-colour ink. Secondary actions use the surface ground; quiet actions have a transparent ground.
- Buttons and native selects have a 44px minimum height. Icon-only controls have a 44px minimum width and an accessible action name. Dense layout never shrinks the interactive surface with a transform.
- Keyboard focus uses a 3px focus-colour outline with 4px offset. Scrollable rails must leave the focused control visible. Hover is tonal; pressing may translate a control by 1px.
- Selected filters use the text ground and background-colour ink with `aria-pressed`. Qualification buttons preview a placement; they are not commitment buttons.
- Native exact-season selects carry exact season IDs and display the year without the redundant team name. Never replace their values with year-only IDs or a fixed number of demo season buttons.
- Loading, failed data, empty search, hidden blocked cards, and unavailable qualifications are distinct states. Show truthful copy and the relevant retry or reveal action. Never fabricate data to fill a visual gap.

## Imagery

Use free reusable sources first, retaining per-file source, credit, licence, and verified capture-year metadata. Prefer photos from the selected year; otherwise choose the nearest verified photo from the player's playing career and show its actual year. No current-photo-as-historical claim. No fabricated faces. Missing images receive an intentional no-photo treatment. Current franchise marks must not be presented as verified historical marks. Copyright status and trademark restrictions are distinct.

Team marks fit wholly inside their padded tiles, preserving aspect ratio without cropping or intrinsic image dimensions expanding the layout. Reviewed round source marks retain circular frames; square/freeform marks use square frames across era fronts, backs, and interface tiles. Missing reviewed marks retain labelled abbreviation fallbacks.

All club marks identify actual franchises rather than the app's own brand. Reviewed non-free marks may be included in the UI and exported season share images under an asserted fair-use basis, with accurate source/copyright disclosure and a non-affiliation notice. Do not describe those marks as freely licensed or permission-cleared. Portrait and atmosphere reuse requirements are unchanged. There is no standalone logo download or card-printing feature.

## Interaction and Responsive Behaviour

### Card rules

- When a surface can show cards, it shows cards. Rosters, stacks, pools, and collections render card tiles (a compact tile where space is tight) rather than text rows; empty places show a dashed card frame.
- When a card can move like a real object, it does. Sorting, filtering, searching, dealing, taking, and cutting animate cards from where they were to where they go; nothing reorders in a cut. Motion off and reduced motion still cut immediately.
- Card lists browse the same way everywhere through `CardBrowser`: search, a Sort by switch (choosing the active sort again reverses it where direction matters), and filter chips with counts in a rail beside the cards on wide screens, and above them on phones with the filters behind a toggle.

### State and commitment

Permanent exact-season picks, qualification-aware browsing, local resume, lineup order, worker simulation, all 162 game details, sharing, and retries remain available. DraftBoard owns transient candidate selection, exact-season choices, placement previews, movement, and inspection. Selecting a candidate highlights engine-approved empty destinations; a single destination previews automatically but never commits. A field preview identifies the full player, exact season, and destination: a small accent label (Preview · slot, or Selected before a destination), then the name with the year in muted text. Only explicit Draft confirms a permanent pick. Back clears the destination; Cancel selection, sheet dismissal, or clicking outside the card clears transient state without changing the roll or roster. Card controls, field slots, and confirmation controls preserve the active selection. A failed session mutation retains the preview and reports its error in a modal error dialog that stacks above any open sheet or dialog, so the header and page never shift. Dismiss, Escape, and the backdrop close it; Retry and Start new draft appear when they apply. Selecting a season that previews on the field dims that candidate's art to 70%; other candidates dim to 56%.

Move first selects a committed hitter on the first click and raises its front for review on the second click. Review first opens the front immediately and offers Move card on the reverse. Movement highlights only engine-approved moves or reciprocal swaps. These assignments apply immediately, preserving the current roll and any unaffected legal candidate destination. Pitching tiles review rather than reassign; rotation order stays in the lineup editor. Inspection never replaces a pending candidate or shifts the field. After fourteen picks the existing lineup editor takes over.

### Stadium choice

New draft during an unfinished draft opens a modal confirmation (Leave this roster behind?) over a dimmed backdrop, with focus on Keep draft. Escape, the backdrop, and Keep draft leave the draft untouched; Discard and start new replaces the saved run. The page behind never moves.

A new draft opens on the stadium deck before the first roll; choosing a stadium lands on the foil pack. The deck shows all thirty current parks as radio cards: two columns on phones, three from 48rem, four from 72rem. A Stadium / Team pill toggle sorts by park name or by club name. Each card's top-left star favorites the park; favorites sit first under either sort, and cards slide to their new places when the order changes. Sort and favorites are kept per browser under `162-zero:stadium-deck:v1`; when storage is blocked the deck says they will reset. Each VenueCard is a 5:7 card in the player-card format on the franchise's colour stock: the park's ballpark photo with its year chip (or a striped No ballpark photo panel), a nameplate with the franchise mark, park name and franchise, and LF/CF/RF distance tiles. Clicking the card chooses it; its turn button shows the back without choosing. The back holds the field-outline SVG with the diamond, distances, wall-height range, roof, elevation, and the photo's credit and licence; the same turn icon in the back's corner turns it back. Estimated distances carry `est.` and unsourced elevation says so. The selected card gets an accent outline and a Selected tag. A sticky footer names the selection and holds Draft at this stadium, disabled until a park is chosen; its background extends below the bar so browsers that pin it above a collapsed bottom toolbar (Firefox on iOS) show no cards beneath it. Stadium cards show physical facts only, never ratings or bonuses.

### Desktop drafting

Wide mode starts at 1100px, or 1024px in landscape. The draft shell has a 1440px maximum width and 24px horizontal gutters. A single sticky header combines the wordmark, compact team-colour roll identity and bounded year range, pick count, Rules & model, and New draft.

Each roll starts from a sealed foil pack centered in the browsing area, set low enough that the dealt card's rise clears the sticky header. Opening it commits the roll immediately, tears the seal, and deals one team card; the pack pulls down and away while the card flips to show the franchise mark, name, and bounded year range on the team colour. The card waits, focused, until the player takes it; a faded extra pack to its right, matching the sealed pack's foil and seals, names the extra packs left. It shows as the pack opens, dimmed and inert until the dealt card lands, so repeated taps never add a pack early. Tapping it deals another card out of it at once: the seal tears and the card pulls up out of the pack's top, face down, smaller than the pack and behind it; once the spent pack has dropped clear the card comes forward, grows to full size as it settles beside the others and flips face up, the cards already out sliding over to keep the row centered. Lifted dealt cards and the ghost keep one visible gap between them. Up to three cards share the column's width, shrinking on phones. A jackpot pack deals as a gem-holo card with a star, Jackpot, and its count of elite seasons instead of a franchise mark; while it is the roll, the header shows a gem-holo star tile in place of the team mark, Jackpot, and the season count, and the pool lists its elite seasons from any franchise. The pack and card teach themselves: their caption (Tap the pack, then Take the card, or Take one pack) fades in only after 5 seconds untouched. Taking a card slides it onto the header roll identity while any other cards drop out the bottom, which stays concealed until the card lands, and the franchise name and era then grow sideways out of the mark. Once the card has landed and the pool has loaded, the candidate cards deal out of the team mark into the grid: each on-screen card flies from the mark to its slot in reading order, small and alternately tilted at first, then settles flat. Up to twelve cards fly, 45ms apart; the rest appear in place. The sealed pack carries a coin that slowly turns through the league's franchise marks, changing marks each time it passes edge-on; opening the pack whips it to fourteen times speed while the seal tears and the card pulls. With motion off or reduced motion the coin holds still. Confirming a desktop pick first clears the stage: on-screen candidate cards drop out the bottom in reading order, 30ms apart, while the browser fades; the pick then commits and the next sealed pack rises into place. Confirming a phone pick in the field sheet presses the previewed card into its slot; the sheet then closes with the page at the top, the card drops into the Your field button, which bumps, and the next pack rises. A failed pick returns the grid at once. With motion off or reduced motion, the card appears face up, taking it shows the roll in the header at once, and the grid appears without a deal.

Card motion is continuous. Moving or swapping a card between field slots slides it from its old slot to its new one, and a newly previewed candidate flies from its grid card into the slot above the field panel rather than clipped inside it; its caption fades in on landing. C and DH names stay on one line so their captions never spill past the field window. Raising a roster card crossfades the compact miniature into the full front as it travels, and returning reverses the crossfade. These motions follow the card animation and speed settings.

Action feedback (copied, downloaded, saved, failed) appears as a toast at the bottom of the viewport rather than a paragraph that shifts the page. One action's progress and outcome replace each other in a single toast; errors stay longer and are announced assertively; a message carrying a link to copy by hand stays until dismissed. Loading placeholders stay inline.

Screens hand off rather than cut. Choosing a stadium drops the rest of the deck out the bottom while the chosen park stays; its card then shrinks onto the field's stadium card as the draft header and field panel rise in. The fourteenth pick clears the candidates as usual, then every field card flies from its slot to its lineup row, each row's details rising in behind its card. Simulate drops the lineup rows away and the season broadcast builds up line by line. When the season finishes or the reveal is skipped, the broadcast's record carries the final record onto the results scoreboard and the rest of the results rise in beneath it. Each incoming screen starts at the top of the page, and the next screen's code loads during the current one so no loading frame interrupts a hand-off. A departing screen that does not change (a failed action) returns at once. With Motion off or reduced motion, every hand-off is an immediate cut.

### Motion and feedback

Mouse and pen tilt cards under a fixed directional light. On phones and tablets, touch does not tilt; the motion sensor does, through Tilt cards with your phone in Settings (`src/lib/cards/device-tilt.ts`): tilt is measured from a neutral pose that slowly follows the grip, so a phone held still at any angle shows cards at rest, and the screen's rotation is accounted for. It is on by default except where the browser asks for motion permission (iOS), which the switch requests on the tap that turns it on; a saved choice on iOS asks again on the first tap after a reload. Reduced motion, Motion off and a hidden page stop it, and a fine-pointer-only desktop never offers it. Every full-size card front carries its own light canvas: one shared WebGL2 context renders the card's surface map under its current light and hands each frame to that canvas, redrawing pointer-driven light every frame, ambient light about every 80ms, and resting cards once. Draft candidate cards and Results award cards opt into autonomous drift/finish lighting, which a hovered card hands over to pointer tilt; it adds sweeps and spotlights. Compact cards, thumbnails and share captures keep the static, fixed-light finish. Home wall cards render their finish as the flat, fixed-light stack that share capture uses, and one WebGL2 pass over the wall supplies their moving light: each card's light sweeps across it on its own rhythm, one card at a time catches a spotlight, and the pass redraws after each marquee step so light stays registered to its card. Without WebGL2 the wall keeps the static finish. Passive drift and finish lighting run whatever the Motion setting, and stop only for system reduced motion. One roughly 30fps scheduler serves all autonomous effects, pauses hidden/offscreen subscribers through shared visibility and intersection observation, and freezes logical time while the document is hidden. Settings persists Motion, Card review animation, Card speed, Tilt cards with your phone, and Roster first click in `162-zero:settings:v1`. Motion governs all movement and transitions except passive card drift and finish lighting; the card-animation switch can disable review travel and rotation separately. Card speed ranges from 0.5× to 2×, with 780ms at the default 1×. Ambient amount remains 2.5× and ambient speed 1×. Storage denial leaves controls usable for the session. Live system reduced motion overrides animation without changing saved preferences and restores a fixed readable light pose. Card flights lift, scale, and rotate from live source bounds; return flights reach those bounds before restoring inline layout. Disabled card animation exposes the centered reading face without travel or rotation. Animate transforms and opacity, never width or grid layout.

Card flights use the reader’s measured layout, including reserved scrollbar space, so the last raised frame aligns with the inline source. Compact source artwork remains intact during travel and resolves to the canonical full front when centered. Reverse layout and controls remain mounted through the return flight. Flights remain clipped beneath the sticky draft header until the card clears it.

Card surfaces are described per pixel by a surface map painted with Canvas2D from the card's laid-out DOM: relief, metal, gloss and iridescence. Templates tag elements with `data-surface` (`stamp`, `name`, `mark`, `frame`, `photo`, `ring`, `sticker`, `ink`), and `src/lib/cards/surface/surfaces.ts` decides how each kind is pressed and coated for the card's finish tier and era. Base cards get only debossed frames; Foil foil-stamps the name; Foil + emboss raises the name and mark; Gem adds holo. Every card sits on its era's stock: uncoated fiber for the 1950s–60s, matte for the 1970s–80s, glossy UV for the 1990s, and chrome from the 2000s. Stickers are raised vinyl; foil stickers are metallic. Photos stay flat under a gloss coat. The light pass derives normals from relief, so lettering, marks and plates catch light and cast shade without extra DOM layers. Map lettering is placed from the browser's own glyph boxes, so tabular numerals, kerning and wrapping register with the printed text. Raised names take a relief profile: pillow, ridged rim, engraved groove, or etched lines; the Cards/Surface Storybook workshop compares them.

Home’s decorative wall is independently clipped and rotated −7°. Desktop renders four duplicated 176px rows with 22px gaps; phone renders three 96px rows with 12px gaps in a 300px top band, so copy starts below rather than competing with the cards. A left-to-right and top/bottom scrim protects text, and the masked logo strip uses all thirty real franchise states. The wall is inert to pointer and assistive technology. Results award fronts alone use continuous idle motion; compact hand cards remain still and lift only for hover/focus.

Neighboring wall rows use disjoint era palettes, and each row separates matching eras through its repeat seam. Each showcase player belongs to one row and appears once per base cycle, even when several seasons are available. Full cycles repeat to cover the viewport; a finite showcase can repeat players within a row on wide screens.

A screen whose code or data is still loading shows its skeleton: the same layout as the loaded screen, drawn in bones, so nothing moves when the real screen replaces it. Every screen has one (stadiums, the draft board with its sealed pack or its player pool, lineup, season, results, and Draft Mode's entry), and the draft board's pool reuses the pool skeleton while eligible seasons load. Skeletons use the shared primitives in `layout.css`: `.sk` blocks, `.sk-card` 5:7 cards, `.sk-line` text lines that take the surrounding font size and line height, `.sk-panel` surfaces and dashed `.sk-outline` slots and ghosts; the draft skeleton outlines every field slot and the extra-pack ghost. Bones are `--surface-raised` on the page background, never invented names, numbers or players. ### Lineup, season, and results

The lineup editor separates batting order, starting rotation, and relief (closer and RP1–RP3). Drops, cancellations, and up/down moves settle every row into its slot with the same motion; Motion off places rows without travel. Escape, pointer cancellation, or dropping outside the list leaves order unchanged. Labelled up/down controls and live announcements provide keyboard ordering. The closer and named relievers remain fixed and labelled CL and RP1–RP3. Added to your collection sits below the rest of the hand (in the same column beside the share panel from 64rem). `/collection` uses the 84rem shell with the same back link, heading and lede as My seasons. ### Sharing and publication

Results shares 1080×1920 images from the `ShareArtwork` component, sized so a phone shows every name legibly. A header holds the wordmark and home park, the labelled final record, and the grade badge with its win band; a ruled strip shows runs scored, runs allowed, first loss and longest win streak; a one-line invitation, Beat this at 162-0.dev, closes the frame. Between them, the Field layout (`ShareField`) is the draft field card drawn from the home park's measured outline in its franchise colour, every roster card in its slot as a real compact Card front captioned with the surname and slot · year. Its relievers sit under SP1–SP3, and the grade ladder (one column of tiers, best on top, the earned band lit) takes the closer's column beside RP3. The Cards layout (`ShareFan`) is a 4×5 grid of large card fronts: the lineup in batting order, SP1–SP3, CL, RP1–RP3, the home park, and the grade ladder (three columns of tiers spanning the last row) with the earned band lit; each card is captioned with initial and surname and its order or role and year. A Field/Cards segmented control above the preview chooses the layout for the preview, download and clipboard image. The preview is a real image from the start, drawn on the device as soon as Results opens. The image never prints the replay URL; the link travels with the share itself. The published preview, download and clipboard image resolve to the stored PNG bytes of the chosen layout, and replay and crawler metadata use the field image; capture uses a fixed readable finish-light pose and no ambient animation. The share fan stamps each card's draft pick number on its corner. Every other page, and any roster link without a publication, previews with `static/og.png`: a 1200×630 frame with the wordmark and pitch on the left and seven era cards fanned and tipped back on the right.

The first Challenge, Copy link, Download PNG, or Copy image action stores the current replay, then draws the artwork off screen in this browser, inside a 1080px query container so its `cqw` lengths never follow the device viewport, and uploads both PNGs with the season record. Capture waits for fonts, every image (forced to load eagerly), and settled card fitting; any failure leaves the replay stored and offers retry. The server stores the first publication for a replay immutably and never recomputes it. Native sharing may need a second tap after preparation; denied/unsupported clipboards expose a selected readonly link or Download PNG instead of reporting success.
