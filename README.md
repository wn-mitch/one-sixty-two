# 162-0

A browser baseball challenge: draft sixteen exact historical selections (nine hitters, three starters, a closer, and three named relievers), then play a deterministic 162-game season. SvelteKit 3, Svelte 5, Tailwind 4, and Cloudflare Workers Static Assets. Season simulation and share-image capture run in the browser; the Worker only checks and stores replays and published images. No user account or server database is required; sharing uses the configured R2 object store.

Planned shared simulation and Draft Mode work is organized in the [design index](design/README.md), with authoritative system rules and proposed implementation phases.

Draft Mode is unfinished and developer-only. Development servers and builds with `VITE_DRAFT_MODE=on` include it; elsewhere `/draft?developer=on` opts a device in (and `?developer=off` out). Public visitors to `/draft` see a return-to-Play page, and Draft Mode history in My teams is hidden while saved runs are kept. When enabled, it is playable at `/draft`: choose a franchise and AI difficulty, draft from passed piles against seven AI clubs, repair the roster in the opening window and submit it under the cap, then play an eight-club, 162-game league season on Classic's season broadcast in eighteen nine-game segments, changing the lineup and relief roles at each stop; every third stop closes one of six chapters. Drafting uses the Classic draft board: the seat's two packs open into the first pile, each pick is placed in an open slot or on the bench and can be moved later, the pile sorts by WAR, OPS / ERA, salary or deal order and filters by position, and a League tab shows every club's roster and payroll and each AI pick with its reason. The opening window and every season stop use Classic's lineup screen: the window sets the field, bench, batting order, rotation and relief roles, and Submit roster carries that whole lineup into the season. At each stop each lineup row sets the card's own line beside its season so far, tinted when it runs well above or below the card, and Smart lineup, Ace first and Smart roles apply Classic's orderings. The opening window runs in two steps. First the market: AI clubs release what they cannot keep to waivers and each may send one trade offer; the player proposes up to three cards each way to any AI club (three offers per club), and the club accepts, counters once with the smallest change it would take, or turns it down, judging by its own scouting and asking more of a player whose projected payroll is over the cap. AI clubs never trade with each other. A traded card cannot be traded again for 54 games, and the deadline stays with the card through release and claim. Cuts go on waivers, where the player marks cards to claim. Close waivers resolves every club's claims once, lowest drafted value first, one card per club per pass while it can still afford a full roster of commons; unclaimed cards leave for good and never appear as cleanup. Then cleanup: cuts are final, cleanup commons and Auto-fix fill the roster, and AI clubs submit theirs. Each club's fans start out expecting the winning percentage its active payroll promises, and after every nine games their mood moves with wins against that expectation (more for fair-weather Los Angeles and New York, plus a swing for games against a recognized rival); the expectation drifts toward the record, then resets to it once the gap is obvious. Mood fills seats, and a fuller, happier home crowd leans the strike zone toward the home side (home hitters walk more and strike out less), within a home record of roughly .513 to .574 against .540 for a neutral crowd; a hostile crowd turns its share against its own club, and larger crowds add game-to-game volatility that favors neither side. Every stop pays each club a fixed salary, a gate from its home games' filled seats, and a bonus for each win that grows with the run margin, and the cash banks for the run. Every stop opens a training shop: hitting, pitching, fielding and baserunning basics (10 each) and two seeded specials from power, plate discipline, contact, strikeout pitch, control, keep it in the park, stamina, recovery, cross-training and nine-game hot bat or hot arm. Cross-training teaches a hitter one new position (never catcher) at 15 points of glove below his best glove that leads there, 30 moving up to short or center; the position stays with the card, counts for lineups, trades and roster legality, and shows with a + beside his positions. Each training moves one skill part of the way to an elite ceiling taken from the card pool, so gains shrink as the skill improves; a hitter keeps their own mix of walks, singles and homers. Any rostered card, reserves included, may take one training per stop; upgrades stay with the card and record who paid. AI clubs buy hitting and pitching basics for their active cards by expected gain. Lineup rows show a trained card's OPS change and a badge counting its lasting trainings. `engine league-crowd` (`cargo run --release -p engine-cli -- league-crowd --edges 0,0.05,0.1 < request.json`) measures the home record and win margins at fixed crowd edges over many native league seasons. Every third stop opens a chapter market before the shop: the cap rises 3%, a seeded market condition (such as a bat premium or a buyer's market) reprices unowned cards, and every club gets a supplemental pack of four fresh non-common cards, dealt best card first in reverse standings order, from which it may sign one that fits its cap at the condition's price, which stays its salary. Trades, cuts and one waiver round then run as in the opening window, with claims in reverse standings order and AI clubs signing from their packs when waivers close; trade cooldowns count from the market's game, so a card traded at game 27 is tradeable again at game 81. AI clubs take default lineups for their new rosters, and each club's fan expectation moves with its new payroll. Each chapter plays under one decree from Mad Fred, the commissioner, drawn by the run seed without repeats from the eight the engine supports: Last Call (seven-inning games), Overtime Mandate (twelve), Four-Out Baseball, Two-Out Express, Singles Appreciation Night (every hit is a single that moves each runner one base, and nobody steals), No Free Passes (a walk or hit batter takes second and runners move up two bases), Double Jeopardy (a double play ends the half inning) and Everybody Tags (every runner moves up on a caught outfield fly that is not the last out). The engine plays every league game under the decree, and setup men and closers follow the last regulation inning. The opening decree shows beside the sealed packs and in the opening window, each later one at the head of its chapter market before any spending, and the decree in force at every stop; the rest of the calendar stays hidden. A league request's `rules` field sets the same rules for `engine league-crowd`. Injuries come only from pushes a stop offers with their risk and the player who would cover: riding a starter two innings deeper, skipping a closer's or setup man's rest, training intensively (half as much gain again) and playing a minor injury through. Each carries 1 in 8, plus 1 in 16 for each earlier push on the same player that chapter; a quarter of injuries keep the player out for the next nine games, and the rest sit him unless he plays hurt at reduced strength. The best healthy reserve who can play the slot covers an absence, else an emergency call-up common who leaves at the next stop. AI contenders ride their ace and rest-skip their closer; others train intensively. A franchise package earns its place by being worth roughly five to ten wins a season to a roster built around it, measured natively. Franchise packages are explained when the franchise is chosen and at every stop. Texas gains half as much again from strikeout-pitch training (on offer at every stop), and Detroit 5% more from any training per common on its active roster, up to 40%. Five franchises trade money: Atlanta's cap is 50% higher for a smaller, quieter crowd; Kansas City's is 10% tighter but every home win pays 8 cash; Oakland's is 12% tighter but every training costs 55% less; Baltimore's is 10% tighter, and at each payday every hitter who batted in that stop gets a free hitting training that grows with his streak of stops, up to ten. St. Louis's fielders all reach balls quicker, home and away, for each active fielder whose printed glove grades 60 or better at his position. Each is measured or estimated at about 5–9 wins a season net. Pittsburgh's cap is 10% tighter, and trading away a card it paid to train banks twice that spending when the chapter market closes, once per card; the trade desk shows each card's bonus and every stop the season's total. Toronto's dollar turns each chapter, three chapters strong and three weak in a seeded order: a strong dollar makes training 40% cheaper, thins the home stands by 15 points of fill and raises ticket prices 30%; a weak one makes training 40% dearer, fills the stands 15 points more and cuts tickets to 1/1.3. Every stop shows this chapter's rate and the next, so a club can bank cash for a strong chapter. The Angels relocate every chapter: their home games run under one of the five built park conditions in a seeded order with no repeat until all five are used, with twice that park's familiarity edge, and every stop shows this chapter's park and the next. Houston's batters read the visitors' signs at home: 0.25 more strike-zone lean and 2.5% faster batted balls, full in a visitor's first game there each chapter and halved for each later one; `engine league-crowd --information CLUB=x` measures it. Five more franchises play at exaggerated parks with their own familiarity rules: Arizona's baked infield sends ground balls off the bat 15% faster (infielders whose printed glove grades 60 or better know it); Miami's swamp slows ground balls 12% and takes 0.35 off every runner's speed skill (fast runners, speed 0.7 or more); Tampa Bay hangs four catwalk rings over the field, the upper two live and catchable, the lower two home runs (20-homer hitters); Milwaukee's fans ferment from the seventh inning on, when batted balls fly 3% harder and every fielder's hit prevention drops 0.3 (20-homer hitters); and Minnesota runs cold, thaw, warm, warm, cool, cold, the cold cutting starters' out budgets to 70% and thickening the air (strikeout starters), with each stop showing the weather now and next. Cincinnati's outfield ends in a steep terrace 10 m deep in front of every fair fence: a ball rolling onto it runs back down, and a fielder climbing it runs three extra meters for each meter up (fast outfielders, speed 0.7 or more, know it). Washington votes at every chapter stop on the next chapter's groundskeeping: funded grounds send ground balls off the bat 10% faster and let them roll farther (infielders whose glove grades 60 or better know them), a shutdown's long grass slows them 12% and stops them sooner (fast runners); an AI club, or a player who does not vote, takes the state its active lineup knows better, and the vote locks when the chapter's games start. Each moves scoring at its park by roughly 5–25% and is worth about 8–13 wins a season to a roster built for it (two native drafts each). A training's franchise and intensive multipliers are fixed when bought and stay with the card. Five franchises play at exaggerated parks (`engine/src/park_package.rs`): Colorado's thinner air, Seattle's heavy air, Boston's left-field wall 2.5 times taller, the Yankees' shorter right field and San Francisco's wind toward right. Each moves scoring there by 17–21% for both clubs. The home club also holds a familiarity edge for each active player whose printed card knows the park: left-handed or switch hitters for the Yankees and Giants, right-handed hitters for Boston, hitters with 20 or more homers for Colorado, and pitchers with at least a strikeout an inning for Seattle. At home its batted balls leave the bat faster and the visitors' slower, by up to 2%; each stop shows the count and the edge. A roster built for its park gains about 7–10 wins a season, a roster drafted without regard to it about 1–5 (40 paired native seasons each). `engine league-crowd --park CLUB=package` (or `=none`) measures a park's runs a game and home record over paired native seasons, `--familiarity F` sets that club's edge, and `--club CLUB` reports that club's whole-season record as `clubWinPct`. AI park fit still values cards for the real parks. The finished season's report grades the finish rather than the win total, since the clubs start near parity: S+ for a champion ten or more games clear, S for the champion, then AA, A, B, C, C, D and F down the table, and Perfect at 162-0; My teams shows the same grade. It pays one token, another for a top-half finish and another for the title; puts every card on the final roster in the collection binder with the club's record, and stamps each with one league sticker (a won rival series, the title with that franchise, or 162-0); and shows the season's best and worst games with their line scores and the decree they were played under: walk-off wins first, then the biggest comeback, then the widest win, and the mirror image for losses. Draft games play under decree rules, so these moments have no win-chance chart. Rosterings in a Draft season count toward a card's times-drafted stickers. The report's Share block publishes a read-only snapshot of the season (standings, moments, final roster, each chapter's decree, and the run's seed and versions) to the replay bucket under `draft-reports/v1/` with a 1200 by 630 card image the browser draws; `/d/<id>` shows it with the card as its link preview, and Download card saves the same image. The snapshot is content-addressed, so the same season always gets the same link; the Worker accepts only seasons from the current build but keeps showing older ones, since nothing is re-simulated. Five fan bases carry a season-long hype meter (`engine/src/crowd.rs`), filled in home and road games by their trigger: Cubs homers, Cleveland's scoreless innings in the field, White Sox runs in innings without a homer, Dodgers wins, and the Padres' closer entering with a lead. At home the meter adds to the crowd's strike-zone lean from the next half inning, up to +0.15; it keeps 90% of itself after every game, carries between stops and is shown at each stop. The Dodgers' home lean also grows 2.5 times with a lead and falls to 0.3 times when trailing in the last three innings of regulation. Each is worth about 5.5–6.5 wins a season to the club that drafted it (40 paired native seasons each); `engine league-crowd --trigger CLUB=trigger` measures one. The other franchise packages are not built yet.

Draft progress, fielding/DH assignments, lineup order, and relief roles are saved on the device. Exact season selections are permanent; qualifying position players can move into an empty slot or swap reciprocally before simulation. Moves preserve a pending roll and must leave the roster completable. Each draft may add two extra packs from its seeded stream: either both to one roll, for a choice of three, or one each to two rolls. A device's first Classic draft (no completed season, once per browser) carries one bonus extra pack, recorded in its replay as `bonusPack`; a roll still holds at most three packs. The player keeps one open pack and the others are discarded. Jackpot packs deal six elite seasons from any franchise and decade instead of one franchise-decade: two of each draft's sixteen rolls (at seeded positions) are jackpots, and every other roll or added pack is one with a 20% chance. Each jackpot balances its six choices across open slots in seeded order, drawing from the eight most valuable remaining legal players at each slot and taking each player's best eligible season. Only seasons whose unslotted card prints in the Gem finish are eligible (`jackpotSeasons` in `scripts/data/compile.ts`), so every jackpot card is a gem; a slot with no remaining gem gets no jackpot choices. Already drafted players and franchises remain excluded. Previously shown, undrafted players may return when a slot has no unseen candidates; a player appears only once per pack. Thus one remaining slot gets six choices and two get three each when enough legal players remain. A season taken from a jackpot keeps its own franchise and decade, which then count as used, and the pick is marked `jackpot` in saves and replays. Replay schema 8 with model `contact-v3` and rules `classic-v8` is the only supported gameplay contract. Sharing stores versioned inputs, including the chosen home stadium and its geometry version and the bullpen's relief roles, plus the authoritative roll/added-pack/pack-choice/pick/reassignment action history under an opaque replay ID; the recipient validates and recomputes that history, while the Worker checks only its shape, versions, size, and current `dataVersion` (from the small `/data/current.json` pointer) so each request stays within the Workers Free CPU limit. The `REPLAYS` R2 bucket stores replay inputs and immutable publication assets, not accounts or request metadata. Incompatible saves and links remain detectable, explain the beta cutover, and require a new draft instead of being silently reinterpreted.

Link previews: a published roster link (`/r/<id>`, `/c/<id>`) uses that season's 1200×630 link preview as `og:image`: the record, grade, two season facts and, on a daily run, the daily stamp with its date, beside a two-row collage of the roster's best cards by WAR/162 (earliest picks without rankings). The collage shows up to eight cards and leaves out any below 2 WAR/162 once four stronger ones fill it. Every other page, and a roster link whose images are not published yet, uses the site preview `/og/daily.png`; roster pages are served `no-store`, so a crawler that rescrapes after publication picks up the season image under its own URL. The site preview is the wordmark and one-line pitch beside a fan of era cards from the Home showcase, stamped with today's daily. The Worker stores each day's preview in R2 under `shares/site/daily/`; the first request of a New York day (game pages send one in the background) captures the `/og-image?date=YYYY-MM-DD` page through the Browser Run `BROWSER` binding's screenshot action. Without the binding (local development, browser tests) or when a capture fails, it serves the undated `static/og.png` for a minute and tries again. Regenerate that fallback with the dev server running: `npm run og:capture -- http://127.0.0.1:5173`.

A new Classic draft starts by choosing a home stadium from a deck of the 30 current MLB parks, compiled from `scripts/data/stadiums.json`. Each stadium is a card with the park's photo and line and center distances on the front; turning it over shows the field outline, wall heights, roof, elevation, and photo credit, with estimated measurements labelled. The draft's 81 home games are played there; road games use each 2025 opponent's home park. A stadium has no rating or bonus and affects play only through ball flight. Once a park has 10 counted runs, its card shows other players' typical record there (the median) and the top-10% line, with a small sparkline of their records. The deck sorts by park or club name, and starred favorites are pinned first; both are remembered per browser.

The daily run at `/daily` is one Classic draft per day that every player shares: its seed (`streamSeed(0, "daily:YYYY-MM-DD")`) fixes the packs, jackpot positions and all 162 games, so equal rosters and lineups get equal records, and its home stadium is drawn from the whole deck by the same seed, skipping the stadium choice. The day turns over at midnight America/New_York. A device gets one attempt: starting the daily spends it, the run can then only be resumed, and an unfinished run expires with its day. It has its own save slot beside the Classic draft. The replay carries `daily: "YYYY-MM-DD"`, which validation accepts only with that day's seed and stadium, so results, team cards, share images and shared links show a Daily stamp. Finishing pays 2 tokens (the season token plus a `daily:YYYY-MM-DD` bonus).

A finished daily joins that day's standings. The device sends its first finished season's stored replay ID and record to `POST /api/daily/{date}` with a random 22-character token from the device table `device_identity`, made on first use; the Worker keeps only the token's SHA-256 and takes everything but the record from the replay, which must be that day's seed. One entry counts per device per day (a repeat returns the first entry's standing), and submissions are accepted until two hours after the day turns over. Daily Results and the Home daily panel show "Top 7% of 1,240 daily runs so far" (a lower-half run shows its rank, "#900 of 1,240"; ties share the better rank) and Results draws the day's records in five-win bins with the run's own bin marked, from `GET /api/daily/{date}?wins=N`. `daily_runs.submission` records the server's answer: accepted, or rejected for good (a replay from another game version, or not that day's run); network and server failures retry on the next view. Records are trusted from the client for the percentile and curve.

Every own completed season, daily or not, also counts toward its home park. The device stores the replay and sends its ID, the device token and the win total to `POST /api/stadiums`; the Worker takes the park and versions from the replay and counts each replay once per device (`stadium_runs`), so resubmitting the same season only reads its place. A park's numbers cover the deployed data version with the model and rules of the latest run on it, and the park's latest geometry version, so a release that changes any of them starts every park over. Classic Results show "Top 25% of 412 runs at Truist Park" (a lower-half run shows its rank) with the park's records in five-win bins and the run's own bin marked; under 10 runs the line only counts them. `GET /api/stadiums/{id}?wins=N` answers one park and `GET /api/stadiums` every park's count, median, top-10% line and spread. These records are client-trusted and never verified. Daily, stadium and My teams distributions share one chart (`WinChart.svelte`) with labelled bins, a You marker, a pointer readout and a screen-reader table.

The day's named board at `/daily/{date}` lists only runs the Worker has recomputed: the 25 best by claimed record (wins, then run differential), one row per identical roster and orders at its earliest run with a count of copies, each with its board name or Anonymous and a replay link. Recomputing is on demand: after each standings `GET` for a live day, `waitUntil` checks up to two unverified runs that would reach the board, best claim first. Each check claims its row (`verifying_at`, abandoned after two minutes), validates the full draft history against the deployed manifest, and plays the season with the wasm engine compiled into the Worker (`src/lib/server/daily-verify.ts`, about 0.9 s of CPU). A matching record becomes `verified`; any difference rejects the run, drops it from every count and adds a flag to its device. A player whose run would place sees "Your run is in today's top 25" until it is checked. An optional board name goes with the submission. The device keeps one saved board name (`device_profile.board_name`), set under Settings or saved when a typed name is accepted, and sends it automatically with any run that has no accepted name yet. `src/lib/server/names.ts` refuses a name before it is stored (3–20 letters, digits, spaces and `.-'_`, no links, and a broad term list, including the game's and studio's names, matched through case, accents, look-alike letters, leetspeak, separators and repeats); the response names the reason, which the results page shows, and a refused name leaves the run Anonymous and can be replaced that day. A device with a counted run may report another row's name; three reports from distinct unbanned devices hold it as Anonymous until reviewed. `/conduct` states the name rules.

Draft cards select a candidate without saving a pick. Choose a highlighted field destination, review the exact season preview, then confirm with Draft. Turning a candidate card raises its reverse into a centered reading surface. Field-card review opens centered on the front; the turn symbol beneath it shows the back. Turning back, Escape, or clicking outside returns the card to its original slot without moving the field. Move first selects a hitter for movement, then opens review on a second click. Review first opens review immediately and offers Move card on the back. Phones and portrait tablets use a field sheet with Field/Card back tabs and pinned confirmation. Text version belongs to the reverse face and presents the same statistics and source credits in readable text, without supplemental estimates or model explanations. Shared rules and model assumptions live on Rules & model. Clicking outside cards, field slots, and action controls discards only the preview; qualifying roster moves and swaps apply immediately.

Hitters and pitchers share one ranked candidate list, ordered by each player's default exact season: batting or pitching WAR/162 by default, or OPS and ERA, which interleave the two roles by each card's place within its role (`src/lib/components/candidate-ranking.ts`). Choosing another season changes the card, not its rank, and never commits a pick. The pool has no search box. It starts with twenty players and appends another twenty near the end; Load more players provides the keyboard fallback and focuses the first new card. Existing cards stay mounted. When rankings arrive, the visible prefix extends through the selected player. Full roles fold below the complete list. Sort and filter changes retain the gather/reorder motion; loading another batch preserves the current placement.

## Interface and results

Shared navigation connects Play, My teams and Collection, with a labelled Shop link showing the token balance and a Menu holding How to play, Settings, Rules & model, and data and image credits. Below 48rem the header is one row: the three destinations fold into Menu, which marks the current one. Head-to-head runs and results belong to My teams. The dense desktop draft retains its drafting header with Shop and Menu; New draft lives inside the menu. The phone draft toolbar labels Extra pack · N left and Field · picks, and keeps New draft in Draft options. Starting over confirms before discarding progress.

Home leads with "Can you go 162-0?", a one-line pitch, Play now: draft your team, and Resume my team when a draft is in progress; How to play lives in Menu. A first visit (no completed season and no draft in progress) puts Play now above the daily panel as the primary action. Otherwise, until today's daily is finished, its panel (date, home park, Play or Resume today's daily) sits above the Classic buttons as the primary action and they turn secondary; afterwards the panel shows the day's record with its standing among the day's runs, a countdown to the next daily, and See results and Board. See results reopens the day's Results at `/daily` without playing the season back (it is hidden once that season is deleted from My teams or retired by a game update), so Back and Forward move between Home and those Results. `/?build=1` (the empty My teams and Collection action) starts a team unless one is in progress. A device that has never completed a season sees first-season callouts, one at a time, each pointing at what it explains: the goal at the stadium, the pick count at the first pack, cards and WAR/162, open spots, the Field, the extra-pack control, that everything is free (extra packs cannot be bought, tokens are cosmetic, there are no ads; the website adds a Ko-fi "Buy me a pack" link), and the finished roster; the season those callouts guided then gets one on its results. Callouts offer Got it and Skip guidance, count as seen when their target is tapped, hide while a sheet or dialog is open, let taps on their text through, and persist in this browser separately from tokens. Skipping guidance never affects the first-season bonus.

The iOS app marks the document with `data-host="ios-app"` before page scripts run; the website alone shows the footer's Support the developer (Ko-fi) link.

Home uses real canonical profiles for its drifting card wall and eight-era showcase, plus all thirty franchise marks. Desktop era cards use four fitted columns and two chronological rows, up to 128px by default, in a panel to the right of the copy from 68rem; phones keep the same two rows of four at up to 80px. The showcase has no scrolling rail. Roster, staff, and lineup cards use the canonical compact treatment: the same eight decade fronts receive era-specific plates and natural name wrapping on phones, and show their printed fronts from 48rem. The draft field is a card printed from the home stadium's measured outline and scaled to its container: 56-unit artwork with full-name captions on a 424-unit card, so all nine hitters and the five-person staff fit the desktop panel and the phone sheet without scrolling sideways. Other compact collections retain a 72px artwork minimum.

Lineup rows show each player's order number, compact card, and historical stats used to set the order (OBP/SLG/HR for hitters, ERA/SO/IP for starters, ERA/SO/SV for relievers). The order number is the mouse/touch drag handle within batting order or starting rotation. The dragged row follows the pointer and the other rows slide aside to preview the drop; only a completed drop saves the order. Escape or dropping outside the list cancels. Labelled up/down buttons remain available for keyboard ordering, and the closer and RP1–RP3 stay fixed. Tapping a compact card raises it from its row for review, as on the draft field. Hide card restores the activating control without changing selections. Fielding/DH changes apply as soon as a legal slot is chosen, retaining qualification-aware moves and reciprocal swaps.

While a screen's code or data loads it shows a skeleton in that screen's own layout (the draft board shows its header, the sealed pack or the player pool, and the field panel's slot outlines), so the loaded screen replaces it without moving. Skeletons carry no player names or numbers, appear only after 120ms, and stop their sheen when Motion is off or reduced motion is requested. The welcome screen loads the stadium and draft code while idle.

Results preserve the complete batting and pitching totals and all 162 game boxes, reached through a pocket-schedule calendar or a by-opponent season-series view. The awards spread considers every participating individual and features MVP, batting title, fewest runs allowed (RA9), strikeout leader, and LVP; the pooled BP remains in team accounting but is not an individual award candidate. Exact unrounded ties receive chips, one stable winner is featured per category, and multiple awards merge onto one card. Inspection switches between the simulated 162-0 season and the recorded actual season, with hitter and pitcher cohorts ranked separately; missing or invalid source values are unavailable, not zero.

The highlight and lowlight radio calls use deterministic wording variants selected from the recorded play and season seed. Home-run calls vary their sentence structure as well as their flight description; they do not invent pitch counts or events.

Device saves live in a SQLite database (`@sqlite.org/sqlite-wasm`) on the origin private file system, opened by a dedicated worker through the `opfs-sahpool` VFS, which needs no cross-origin isolation. `src/lib/storage/migrations.ts` brings it to the current schema on open; each step runs in one transaction with its `user_version` bump, so a failed step leaves the previous version intact, and a database from a newer release is refused. The first step imports the earlier localStorage saves (`162-zero:v1`, `162-zero:library:v2`, `162-zero:series:v1`), skipping malformed entries, and removes those keys once the import is on disk. The pool admits one tab at a time: a second tab, or a browser without OPFS, plays with a memory database and says its saves will not persist. Settings, the stadium deck order, and an accepted challenge stay in localStorage. A Draft Mode run is saved as its setup (seed, franchise and the rules, salary, data and run-format versions) plus the player's action log, and resumes by replaying that log; a run saved under any other version is refused with the reason rather than reinterpreted. Its league season is kept beside it as lineups and played chapters.

Ordinary returns resume unfinished drafts, not completed results. An incompatible active Classic save is cleared after its notice; an incompatible Draft Mode run is retired from automatic resume while its history stays on the device. A successfully saved completion releases its active slot. If that history write fails, the completed draft remains recoverable instead of being discarded. My teams opens completed Classic replays and Draft Mode reports explicitly.

`/collection` is the binder: every player-season the player has put on a finished team, kept even after its season is deleted from the library, with how often it was drafted, its best team record and that season's line. It can be searched, filtered by the positions a card played, sorted by recency, times drafted, year, WAR/162 or best WAR on your teams, and grouped by team or era; a Stickers view lays out every sticker ladder as an album with earned rungs, blank thresholds and secret single-game feats, and it shows completion against every individual player-season in the game data; team bullpens are collected but do not count toward completion. Cards earn stickers (`src/lib/collection/stickers.ts`, `stickers-v5`) from their saved season lines, game logs and team records, on ladders of tiers: 20–70 HR, 150–250 hits, 100–160 RBI, 20–100 steals, a .350–.500 OBP over 502 PA, the 30–30, 40–40 and 50–50 clubs (home runs and steals), hitting for the cycle once or twice, a three- or four-homer game, one to three grand slams and one to three walk-off home runs, 200–600 strikeouts, a starter under 3.00 down to 1.00 RA9 over 162 IP, a closer under 2.00 RA9 over 30 IP (Lights out), 30, 40 or 50 saves, a perfect closer (no blown save in at least 20 save opportunities), one to three complete-game no-hitters, one or two perfect games, one, three or five complete-game shutouts, a 20-strikeout game, team MVP, 14, 16, 18 or 20 estimated WAR in one season on your team, topped by a 22-WAR Video-game numbers sticker (hitters and pitchers on one ladder; simulated WAR runs high, so the steps are set on each season's best player across the draft policies: 14 is about the median, 16 the top fifth, 18 the top twenty-fifth, 20 about one season in 250 and 22 about one in 5,000), team records every 20 wins to 120, then 130, 140 and every 5 to 160 and 162–0, the season's grade, and being drafted 3 to 100 times. The engine counts grand slams, walk-off home runs (game-ending in the bottom of the 9th or later), and saves, blown saves and save opportunities by the MLB rule (`engine/src/workload.rs`). A card face shows its best grade and its three rarest other stickers, ranked by how often simulated card-seasons earn them (`src/lib/collection/sticker-rarity.json`, rebuilt natively with `just sticker-rarity`); kinds the sample never produced rank rarest, higher tiers first, and loyalty stickers rank last. Each sticker is earned once per player-season and can be moved or hidden on its own card; a face sticker the player has not moved sits in its rank's face slot. Results lists the stickers a season earned for the first time. Stickers are cosmetic: they never enter a draft, a replay or the simulation.

With Best WAR selected, each card or player stack shows its best simulated-season WAR. A stack displays the value used to sort it, even when another season is its chosen cover; a dash means no recorded WAR.

Team MVP is one sticker with a running count of distinct completed-season awards for that exact player-season. Replaying the same saved season does not add an award, and deleting a season from My teams does not remove earned credit. Existing saved MVPs and first-award sticker provenance backfill the count.

The binder opens in Player stacks: one cover per player, with every owned exact season available inside. Set as cover remembers a preferred season without changing its stickers, statistics, or draft eligibility. Search and position filters show a matching season rather than hiding it behind a nonmatching preferred cover. All seasons restores individual card browsing and the Team/Era grouping controls. Composite bullpens and seasons whose player identity is unavailable stay separate.

A complete season earns a grade from fixed win bands (`src/lib/game/grade.ts`): Perfect at 162 wins, S+ 153–161, S 149–152, AA 145–148, A 139–144, B 130–138, C 120–129, D 110–119, F 109 or fewer. With two guaranteed jackpot packs and a one-in-five chance on every other roll, random picks average about 106 wins and mostly grade F or D. Taking the best-looking card for the next slot averages about 136 wins and mostly grades B. Drafting by runs above replacement averages about 142 and mostly grades A, with AA about a quarter of the time; spending extra packs on weak rolls adds about a win and a half. S and S+ also need a lucky season. Results, the share image and library clubs show it, and every card on the roster earns that grade as a sticker; the collection and later drafts show the best grade a card has earned.

Results also show a draft grade: the same bands applied to the wins the roster averages over many seasons, before this season's luck. A logistic fit on the 16 cards' summed runs above replacement (`valueRuns`, which data preparation copies from `draft-cards.json` into each drafted card's profile chunk) gives that average to within about a win of native `just draft-spread` measurements. No roster expects 162 wins, so the draft grade tops out at S+; the best possible roster expects about 155.

A scouting grade sits beside them. Replaying the draft, each pick is compared with the card the candidate list showed first for the same slot by WAR/162, across every pack dealt for that pick. The grade counts how often the player took that card and sums the `valueRuns` gained or lost against it, then names the draft Talent scout, Off the board, By the book or Sharp eye. The bands in `src/lib/game/scouting.ts` come from native `just draft-spread` drafts, which report both numbers for every roster.

Results quote a real phrase from a period sports page under the record, cited and linked to the page it was printed on. `research/season-phrases.md` holds the cited research (205 verified phrases, 1906–2026, each with publication, date, page and source link); `node scripts/import-season-phrases.ts` turns it into `src/lib/news/season-phrases.json`, dropping any row that is unverified, undated or unlinked, and a unit test fails if the two drift apart. The season grade picks the band. A season six or more wins above or below its draft's expectation prefers phrases about overachieving or falling short, and the replay's seed picks within the band, so a replay quotes the same phrase.

Every completed season of the player's own draft is saved automatically, separate from the draft in progress. Daily seasons sit on their own Daily runs shelf, newest day first, above the other teams. Each entry keeps the replay, record, runs, roster, MVP, and every drafted player's simulated season line. My teams at `/seasons` shows them as deck cards with all-time leaderboards and per-club cards and stats. Head-to-head mode selects two decks for a tale of the tape and Play best-of-five; Ctrl- or Cmd-click also pairs decks. Every season is kept until deleted. Entries can be renamed or deleted. Full season results opens the entry's results page from its inline `#replay=` link. Copy challenge link publishes through `POST /api/replays` and copies the short `/r/{id}` link, falling back to the inline `#replay=` link. Entries from an older game version show as retired: roster, record and stats stay visible, but they cannot play head-to-head. Every finished series is recorded (newest first, a replayed series kept once and moved to the top) with both clubs' library keys, so each club shows its head-to-head tally and past meetings. Results link to My teams.

Club cards show the record once, the nickname or home park, run differential, and win rate as a percentage. All-time leaderboards use compact single-row thumbnails that open the saved club; wins stay in the main gallery instead of a duplicate board.

My teams' secondary Use a replay link action accepts two same-site replay links (`/r/{id}` or `/#replay=`), or adds a replay opponent to a selected saved deck. Head-to-head with this team on any result opens setup with that season already as Team A, asking only for an opponent: a saved team or another replay link. Both regular seasons are recomputed to seed the series: more wins is seed 1; equal records give seed 1 to the replay whose canonical key sorts first, and identical replays give it to Team A. The 2-2-1 format has seed 1 hosting games 1, 2, and 5 on calendar days 1, 2, 4, 5, and 7, each at the host's chosen home stadium. Rotations cycle the three starters and closer workload carries across days. Glove and Wheels are omitted when the leader is under 0.05 runs. Highlights and lowlights are the largest win-expectancy swings from Team A's side. ## Run

Use Node >=24.12 and npm. Install with `npm ci`, then `npx playwright install chromium` for headless browser tests. The simulation engine is Rust: install [rustup](https://rustup.rs) (it reads the pinned toolchain and wasm target from `rust-toolchain.toml`) and `cargo install wasm-pack`. Every hook that needs the engine runs `just engine`, which rebuilds the wasm package into the ignored `src/lib/engine/pkg/` only when the Rust sources change. Browser tests run the built app on local workerd with file watching disabled; `PLAYWRIGHT_PORT` selects an isolated port.

```sh
just dev --host 127.0.0.1 --port 5173
just engine
just test
just smoke --seed 162 --policy best
just calibrate --seed 162 --games 10000
just bench --runs 5
just draft-feasibility --seeds 64
just hill-climb --budget-minutes 60
just hill-climb-replay --seed 162
```

`just bench` prints median timings for policy drafts and a full wasm season, then the native engine's preparation, matchup fitting, and full and fast season timings on the same roster, plus a fingerprint of the seed-162 season result. An optimization that should not change simulated values must leave the fingerprint unchanged. `just test` also runs `cargo test --workspace`, whose search test reads the prepared dataset.

`just hill-climb` searches natively for the Classic roster with the most expected wins, ignoring the draft rolls: any sixteen distinct franchises and players, each eligible at its slot, plus a home stadium and both orders. It starts from the best draft-pricing assignment of slots to franchises in the stadium that screens best, then runs an iterated local search (replacements from each slot's top candidates, position, stadium, batting-order and rotation swaps). Proposals are screened on part of a fixed 64-season seed panel and accepted only when the paired mean-win gain's 95% lower bound over the whole panel is positive, with perturbation restarts after a stall. The best roster is then scored on fresh holdout seeds: mean wins with standard error, the win histogram, the best season and its seed, the count of 162-0 seasons, and an estimated P(162-0) from per-game win rates by opponent, venue and rotation spot (assuming independent games). The checkpoint (`--resume`) and `report.json` go to `.cache/hill-climb/`; `just hill-climb-replay --seed N` replays one full season of the reported roster and lists its losses.

`just draft-feasibility` is the headless Draft Mode D01 proof. It needs no browser or profile chunks. For each seed and pick policy, it deals each of eight seats two eight-card packs of real player-seasons (one gold-or-gem slot, two silver-or-better slots and five common-or-better slots per pack, upgraded at the pool's natural finish rates). Each seat cracks its packs into one 16-card pile; seats take one card and pass the pile left until all 128 cards are drafted, so every pile visits every seat twice. Rosters have 18 active roles (Classic's 16 plus RP4 and RP5; every named relief slot takes any card that could close) and two reserves. It drafts with the Draft Mode AI general manager (`ai`) or stand-in stress policies, then runs opening recovery: cuts, then cleanup from each seat's own unlimited per-role stacks of commons, of which only the top card is visible. It checks every submission against the cap and roster rules. Each seeded table seats eight franchises as a player run would, so AI clubs score with their home parks. AI clubs play at `--difficulty rookie|pro|ace` (default `pro`), and the `ai` policy seats one perfect drafter (true values, no bias) at seat 0 and reports where its submitted roster ranks. It also reports how often AI clubs take the highest finish in a pile and how many picks park fit decided, runs named negative fixtures and replays every run. A one-screen summary goes to stdout, the full JSON report goes to `.cache/draft-feasibility/report.json`, and the exit code is 1 when any gate fails. `--policy ai|value|needs|lopsided|random|mixed|all`, `--difficulty`, `--seed-start` and `--report` select the sweep.

The rankings index pins the same source revision and SHA-256 as the statistical compiler. A season whose source rows cover fewer scheduled games than the shortest completed season in the covered window is published as unavailable rather than ranked from a fraction of a season.

The compiler also writes a versioned `draft-cards.json` (`salary-war-v1`, provisional) for Draft Mode. It records each candidate's modeled season runs above its role's replacement level (`valueRuns`): shared-engine linear weights against a league-average opponent in the neutral park, plus fielding runs at the position, over the role's workload, with replacement at the 20th percentile of eligible cards. Baserunning, park fit and platoon effects are not modeled. The salary is a reputation price: the card's public WAR/162 on its role's market line, a least-squares fit of modeled runs on WAR for hitters, starters and closers (`method.market`). Cosmetic finishes come from WAR and never enter the price. Classic does not load this file. Beside it, `draft-park-{franchise}.json` holds each card's park fit (`park-fit-v1`) for that franchise's 2025 home park: the card's contact fit is played out in the park and in the neutral park, the same change for a league-average player in the role is subtracted, and the difference is scaled to the card's priced workload over the home half of the season, in tenths of a run. Positive means the card plays up there. Draft Mode AI clubs value cards with their own park's fit; it is not part of the salary.

The data compiler pins an immutable third-party transport commit and SHA-256 for every source CSV. Changed or missing bytes fail rather than selecting another release. ## Model and data

One Rust engine (`engine/`) plays every game. It is compiled to wasm for the browser's season and series workers, Node scripts, and data preparation (`engine-wasm/`, loaded through `src/lib/engine/`), and natively for benches and search (`engine-cli/`). Transcendental functions come from `libm` rather than the platform, so wasm and native builds produce identical results. Inputs are validated against the TypeScript data contract in `src/lib/sim/` before they cross into the engine as JSON.

AL/NL franchise history from 1950–2025 is translated into a common combined 2025 batting-event environment. Three-year batting and pitching park factors remove the source park from historical rates; simulated venues act only through ball flight. Historical source league priors, coarse park factors, generic platoon effects, inferred pitcher allowed extra-base hits, and explicit runner-advancement probabilities are approximations, not measured historical splits or a professional simulator. Missing sacrifice flies retain available records and yield conservative plate-appearance components; labelled OBP/OPS denominators are not reconstructed historical totals. Position-specific defense combines Lahman fielding evidence with the pinned source's `fld162`: exact-position errors, double plays, assists, catcher SB/CS and fielding outs are smoothed against year/league/position cohorts. Generic outfield evidence is a half-strength fallback only when split LF/CF/RF evidence is absent. DP and assist rates are context-affected proxies, not true opportunity rates, range, throwing velocity, framing, blocking, or proprietary scouting grades.

Each of three selected starters receives 54 starts. The closer and each named reliever hold a relief role and an optional backup role, set on the lineup screen: Closer takes the ninth or later with a lead of 0–3 runs (even over a starter still going); Setup takes the eighth, then the seventh, with that lead once the starter is done; Stopper enters mid-inning from the sixth with a runner in scoring position and a 0–3 lead, replacing anyone but the closer; Long covers a starter gone before the sixth or either team leading by four or more, for up to three innings; Middle takes any other relief inning and may stay a second inning after facing four or fewer batters. A role's primary holders are called first, then backup holders who did not pitch the previous game. If those choices are unavailable, remaining available named arms enter in bullpen order, with primary closers last; their primary role determines outing length. Defaults are CL Closer/Setup, RP1 Setup/Stopper, RP2 Setup/Middle, and RP3 Middle/Long. A named pitcher never works a third straight day, and an outing longer than an inning costs a game of rest plus one per inning beyond the first (two games after two innings, three after three); there is no season cap. Over drafted leagues (`engine league-workload < league-request.json` reports each club's outs by starters, named relievers and the remainder), drafted relievers take about 93% of relief, long and middle men stay near or under 100 innings, and the remainder covers about 30 innings a season. Every relief inning no named pitcher can take goes to the remainder arm (BP): the pooled, BFP-weighted 2025 league relief, the same for every club, with unlimited workload. Each 2025 opponent uses its saves leader as closer and its next three relievers by outs as named relievers with the default roles, and the rest of its relief as its remainder. Neutral handedness and unlimited remainder workload are arcade abstractions, not reconstructed relief-only innings. Team-season bullpen cards from earlier versions stay in saved seasons and the collection but are not drafted. The schedule draws actual 2025 opposition but is not an official club schedule. All 162 games run, including after a loss.

The schedule plays five games against every opponent plus a sixth against twelve of them, as 60 two-, three-, and four-game series: every opponent hosts one series and visits for another, the venues balance at 81 home and 81 away, and no opponent plays two series in a row. The results calendar dates the season from a Thursday opening day with 24 off days at travel breaks and three seed-drawn makeup doubleheaders; dates are presentation only and do not affect play. Opponent starters cycle through their five-player rotations. Other relief identifies the remainder arm in game boxes and season totals. Only schema 8 / `contact-v3` / `classic-v8` is supported; older replay schemas and model/data combinations are rejected rather than migrated or run through a legacy engine.

Each plate appearance samples BB, HBP, SO, or contact from the platoon-adjusted matchup. Contact draws exit velocity, launch angle, and spray from the 2025 Statcast distribution as continuous values. The compiler fits each matchup's contact mix and power shift so that a neutral reference park (330/380/400/380/330 ft, 10 ft walls, 500 ft elevation, `neutral-reference-v2`) reproduces its park-adjusted singles, doubles plus triples, home runs, and outs; triples are best-effort and fits outside tolerance are counted in diagnostics. Flight uses drag and lift with air density from venue elevation and a per-ball carry factor, in fixed calm conditions with retractable roofs open. A home run must clear the actual wall height at its fence bearing. Hits and outs come from a fielder-reach model: fixed starting positions, reaction, speed, catch height, and throws, fitted to 2025 Statcast outcomes. There is no park-factor multiplier at the venue. `src/lib/sim/park.ts`, `flight.ts`, `fielding.ts`, and `contact-profile.ts` hold the model, and the compiler's `APPROXIMATIONS` list states its limits.

Plate appearances account for inherited runners, forced advancement, errors, steals, double plays, and sacrifice flies. Extra innings begin with empty bases. Non-home-run walkoffs stop on the winning run and truncate hit credit to the winning runner's advance; home runs count every runner. A 1,000-PA half-inning or 100-inning bound raises an error instead of inventing a winner. Results retain all inning lines and batting/pitching boxes; worker progress never controls simulation randomness.

On contact, the physical model decides which fielder plays the ball. A bounded, residualized hit-prevention skill changes that fielder's speed and reaction time; outfield throwing changes throw speed. Error avoidance, infield double-play participation, outfield throwing, and catcher throwing then use their applicable evidence independently. Missing evidence is explicitly neutral; it is not an invented league-history talent rating. `DEF est.` on a hitter front is the pre-season, assigned-position estimate of runs saved per 1,458 reference innings. An unavailable aggregate estimate shows — even when independently evidenced component skills still operate. DH fronts retain HR, while pitchers and BP retain WAR/162 · ERA · SO.

Results separately report realized run value. `sim-war-v2` gives hitters batting value + running value + defensive value + 20 replacement runs per 600 PA, and individual pitchers pitching value + 20 replacement runs per 600 outs, then divides by ten runs per estimated win. Values are not clamped at zero. Participation includes batting, pitching, fielding, and running work; the pooled BP remainder arm contributes to conservation/team accounting but has no individual estimated WAR. This context-dependent app estimate is not historical JEFFBAGWELL, fWAR, bWAR, proprietary-equivalent WAR, or a forecast of team wins.
Season highlights and lowlights are actual steal, caught-stealing, or plate-appearance events measured by changes in the challenge team's neutral win probability. The highlight is the largest positive change across all games; the lowlight is the largest negative change only among games the challenge team lost. A perfect season has no lowlight or lowlight trace. Each selected moment retains its own game's win-probability trace. The win model uses the combined league event rates, actual inning/half, outs, occupied bases, and score, but intentionally removes player, pitcher, team, and park strength. Its absorbing half-inning Markov model uses the physical resolver's neutral league transitions in the neutral reference park, including errors, double plays, tag-ups, and runner advancement. Future steals are omitted from the projection even though actual simulated steals are measured as moments. Remaining half-innings and innings are convolved numerically; propagation stops only below 1e-13 remaining mass and rejects failed convergence or invalid mass. An equal-strength tie after regulation or the current extra inning is worth 0.5, with no ghost runner. A completed or skipped half produces an exact 1 or 0 rather than an estimated final-score story.

Turning-point radio copy is original, authored play-by-play assembled from the simulated event: exit velocity, launch and spray angles, the selected fielder, runner advances and outs, and the score. The engine preserves this observation on each saved moment without consuming random numbers or changing play resolution. Wording varies deterministically with the run seed and moment. The Castellanos home-run interruption remains an intentional Easter egg. An error names and displays the responsible fielder; a baserunning out can display the runner rather than the batter or pitcher. Calls distinguish a catch, a ground out, a scoring tag-up, and a runner thrown out; they do not invent pitch counts, pitch types, swing decisions, wall caroms, or fielding gestures. Older saved summaries without flight detail still receive outcome-based copy. The writing approach follows [Scott Franzke’s discussion of radio play-by-play](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/ahead-world-series-phillies-radio-announcer-shares-art-play-play): describe the action clearly and return to the situation, using original language rather than copied signature calls.


The game publishes runs allowed per nine innings (RA9) and outs-based baseball innings, not simulated ERA, wins, or saves whose official scoring is not reconstructed. Share actions create a replay ID, then the browser renders the canonical Svelte share artwork off screen in a 1080px query container, rasterizes two 1080×1920 PNGs (the field layout and the cards layout) and the 1200×630 link preview with `modern-screenshot`, and uploads them with the season record. The Worker checks each PNG's signature and dimensions, the 8 MiB cap, and that the record adds up to 162 games; it does not recompute the season. The replay is stored when Results appear, so Copy link and Challenge a friend release the link without waiting on the images. Starting the simulation loads the artwork's media, rankings, fonts and roster images; once the worker returns the season, the replay is stored and each image is prepared (mounted and cloned in frame-sized slices) in idle time during playback. The one step that cannot be sliced, drawing and encoding each PNG, waits for playback to end, so playback never stalls; a device that a short style probe finds slow leaves all of it until Results. On a typical desktop the images publish about two seconds after Results appear. Copy link, Challenge a friend and the image actions reuse that work. A replay opened from someone else's link is never published from the viewer's device. All three content-addressed images and the immutable publication manifest are committed together (the first publication for a replay wins); subsequent actions reuse those exact bytes, and crawler metadata uses the link preview once it is published. Challenge a friend shares `/c/{id}`, a challenge card with the record to beat, the published image, Draft your team, and a See their season link to `/r/{id}`; Copy link shares `/r/{id}`, which replays the season itself. Accepting a challenge stores it under `162-zero:challenge:v1` and binds it to a draft's seed: a new draft by default, or, when the device has an unfinished draft, the player's choice of Answer with my current draft (which resumes it via `/?resume=1`) or Start a fresh draft. When that draft's season finishes, Results leads with the comparison and Play the best-of-five, which opens `/h2h` with the new club as Team A and the challenger as Team B. Before publication the preview is the same PNG drawn locally, without uploading, so it can be copied or saved at once; the live artwork shows only until it is ready or if the browser cannot draw it. Capture failure preserves the replay and offers retry; clipboard denial offers a selectable link or PNG download rather than false success. This is friendly deterministic sharing, not a tamper-proof leaderboard.
