# Newspaper phrases for a baseball season

205 verified phrases from 23 publications, dated 1906–2026. The modern expansion adds 52 phrases, all in C (18), B (21), or AA / A (13); 40 additions date from 2000 onward. Total band counts: Perfect: 7; S+ / S: 15; AA / A: 26; B: 58; C: 38; D: 34; F: 27. Entries are ordered newest first within each band. Historical coverage remains concentrated in Sporting Life, 1906–1915, with a substantial mid-century gap.

Bands indicate suggested tone for 162-0, not the historical team's literal win total. Perfect contains claims of historic greatness, not claims that the cited club went undefeated. Notes distinguish overperformance, underperformance, fulfilled expectations, and praise qualified by circumstances. Some phrases concern an early-season stretch, a postseason run, a retrospective, or an amateur, minor-league, college, or school team; those qualifications are retained. The modern expansion prioritizes assessments relative to talent, investment, injuries, and prior expectations. An article published before a season is included only when the selected wording describes prior performance. “Unchanged” in the notes means no phrase normalization.

Every main-table entry was checked in an original newspaper page image or the publication's own article text. Original wire-service printing in a newspaper is marked yes; a modern retrospective is also yes when the quoted wording is the retrospective writer's own or a quotation actually printed there. This does not claim to identify the earliest-ever use of each phrase. No secondhand entries are included.

The context column contains a short exact excerpt including the phrase, or a few words immediately preceding it (following it where noted). Fragments intentionally omit sentence boundaries. Layout line breaks and end-of-line word divisions are joined where noted; spelling, grammatical number, meaningful hyphens, and capitalization are retained. Blank page and author fields mean not established. Dates follow the issue or publisher date, with archive-date discrepancies noted. Headline capitalization is transcribed in readable title form except where explicitly retained; this does not change phrase wording.
